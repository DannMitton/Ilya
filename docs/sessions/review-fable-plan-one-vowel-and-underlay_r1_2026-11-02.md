## C. Step 2b against `monotone`

`monotone` (`underlay-seat.ts:120-154`) is a global least-cost order-preserving alignment with free note skips when tokens are fewer, forced seats when tokens are more. A local re-seat of one word "never sliding the rest of the line" contradicts it: the DP already chose the global optimum given the costs; moving one token off its note without re-running changes the neighbours' optimum. Failure modes:

1. **A melisma the print marks with a slur and no extender** (the common case on this file: 46 slur-stop notes, 40 melisma notes without a slur stop, truth count): today every such note is free, so a token drifts onto it, the next token is displaced, and 2b sees "an empty sung note beside a displaced piece" at the wrong place and moves the wrong word. Met only by making the note unfree (finding 1). 2b cannot meet it.
2. **A note homr missed** (8 syllables, M0): the row has one more token than notes; `monotone` forces every note to take one and leaves the surplus `unseated` (`:117-118`). 2b finds an empty note nowhere and does nothing; the shift from the missed note on is a cascade the rule forbids and the plan does not count. Met by: tokens outnumbering free notes by k is itself the alert (14:27 and 14:34 in one), reported per system, never repaired silently.
3. **A word whose ink straddles a barline:** tokens have no bar; notes do (`NotePlace.bar`, used only by `order`, `:181-197`). `monotone` is bar-blind, so no failure here, but 2b's "beside" test, if written per bar, would miss it.
4. **Two tokens on one note by proportion drift:** `oneToANote` merges them (`underlay-words.ts:59-80`), 2b sees a filled note and a hole one to the right, re-seats the hole's neighbour, and the merged note still holds two vowels. Met by refusing the merge (section A).
5. **homr's `imgpos` out of x order inside a bar** (song 4 bars 7, 8, 10; song 2 bars 100, 102; song 3 bar 12; my count: 6 bars in the file): `seatTokens` sorts notes by x (`:162`), so the ordinal and the x disagree, the token lands on the right x and the wrong ordinal, and `writeLyrics` writes it by ordinal (`underlay-words.ts:221`). 2b cannot see it. Met by: a note whose x is out of order with its ordinal in its bar is flagged and its system left to the singer (§0.2's last sentence).

So 2b is unsound as a local patch and unneeded once (1) and (4) are met; the remaining shifts are measurement (finding 2) and homr (5).
