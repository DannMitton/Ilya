## The scenario, as the singer meets it

1. A singer drops a scan of Musorgsky's «Где ты, звёздочка?». The reader fills Lyrics as today.
2. Ilya searches its library, on the device, from what it already has: the Lyrics' first line and longest words, and the Piece fields if the singer typed them (no reader path reads a title today). It finds Grekov's poem with a confidence score.
3. **Confident match:** Poem fills from the library. Poem's standing line names the source, in effect: "Poem from Ilya's library: N. P. Grekov, 'Где ты, звёздочка?', from [edition]. Paste your own to replace it." (strings owed, EN and FR, desk drafts for Dann). The standing line carries one tap, 'Not this poem', which clears Poem and holds the dismissal for the session. Lyrics' receipt line counts what the reconciliation did ("4 words repaired from the poem").
4. **Several candidates or a weak match:** nothing fills and the ratified `intake.view.landedLyrics` line stands. The candidate list with one-tap pick is a later build.
5. **No match:** nothing changes from today.
6. The singer's own poem always outranks the library's: a paste or scan of a poem replaces the library's text (with today's replace guard), and the library never overwrites a Poem the singer filled.
7. Everything downstream runs as designed: sage differences in Lyrics, grey omissions in Poem, the three tiers, the dotted underline on a repair, Revert. The composer's departures (song 1 sets stanzas 1 and 3, with variants) show as differences, which is what Poem and Lyrics exist to track.

## What the library is

- **Scope, first release:** the poems of the Russian art-song repertoire Ilya's singers sing, starting with the songs already in the corpus (row 114's five, the 18 build songs), then the standard repertoire by composer (Glinka, Dargomyzhsky, Musorgsky, Tchaikovsky, Rimsky-Korsakov, Rachmaninoff, and onward). Russian texts only, the author of the Russian text being the test: a translation is a Russian text by its translator (Marshak, died 1964, for Kabalevsky op. 52, ten of the eighteen build songs). A translator still under copyright keeps that text out. A cycle PDF fills Lyrics with several songs; the match is per song, and build 0 is one song per drop.
- **The public-domain test:** each entry records the author's death year, the first publication year, and a status computed from them at build time: `canada` (died 1971 or earlier; the 2022 extension did not revive expired copyright, N.178 item 17), `life70` (died 1955 or earlier, since a life-plus-70 term runs to the end of the seventieth calendar year after death), and `us` (NOT ESTABLISHED: the United States counts from publication, and a text first published after 1930 may carry a restored term; recorded per entry, never computed until read). Russia adds terms for an author rehabilitated after repression and for war service; where known, the entry carries it. Edition apparatus is never copied. Which status the shipped file filters on is Dann's (question 2). There is no worldwide public domain. This is a desk reading of the term rules, not legal advice.
- **Sources:** an edition named for every text. Preferred: Russian Wikisource (public-domain texts with the edition named), the Fundamental Electronic Library of Russian Literature (feb-web.ru, scholarly editions), the Russian Virtual Library (rvb.ru); each source's terms recorded. Wikisource's footer licenses its contributors' work CC BY-SA 4.0 (read 2026-11-03); a public-domain poem is not re-licensed by being transcribed, and the entry copies the poem's text only, never a header, note, or modernization note, and records the page URL and its revision id. Each Wikisource page carries its own public-domain template and often names a web source rather than a print (the Grekov page names az.lib.ru, 1852); the entry records what the page names and does not call it an edition when it is not one. Coverage is thin for the minor romance poets: neither Wikisource's Grekov selection (19 poems) nor az.lib.ru's Grekov index holds «Где ты, звёздочка?», so the scenario's first song has no source today but lieder.net or a print. An entry with no source read is not an entry; the gap list goes to Dann as a list. The minor poets' texts come from Dann's shelf or from Emily Ezust with her permission (asked 2026-11-03 00:28). 'A few thousand poems' is a labour estimate, not a plan. LiederNet (Emily Ezust) as a cross-reference for which composer set which text and its variant notes, and its texts only with her permission (WebFetch declines to transcribe song texts from it; the desk does not route around that).
- **Each entry:** poet and dates; title and incipit; the text in the edition's spelling, with a modern-orthography form (the LIMIT's substitution, Grayson p. 291) and a normalized form for alignment; alignment, the tiers, and the colouring of differences compare normalized letters, so a pre-reform poem beside a modern Lyrics does not colour every word (which form Poem shows is question 3); source, edition, URL, licence; the settings known (composer, title, opus, year, LiederNet id); a version stamp, and the document records which library version filled Poem, so a corrected text can be re-offered.
- **Several versions of one poem** may be carried (an early and a revised text); the match picks the one that aligns best and says which.
- **Packaging:** listed in `dictionary-manifest.json` under its own fields (`poemFiles`, `poemHash`), never under `hash`: every cache key the loader writes derives from `manifest.hash` (`loader.ts:716`, `:546`), and `cleanLegacyCache` keeps only `dict-{hash}` and `gloss-{hash}` (`:314-320`), so a library keyed to the dictionary's hash would force a full dictionary re-download on every poem added, and a library under its own prefix is never cleaned unless the prefix is added there (item 7's table of spellings has the same gap). The file is fetched whole, never a shard chosen by the match (a per-song fetch would tell the server which song). Its name passes the service worker's dictionary exemption (`sw.js:100`), or the exemption is widened, so it is not stored twice. It is fetched in the background after the app shell, as the HEIC decoder is (`sw.js:30-48`), but into the loader's IndexedDB, not the worker's cache, which every deploy deletes (`sw.js:58-68`). The brief states the byte count (`CONTRACT.md` §8). The manifest pattern is `loader.ts:113-120`, `:337-343`, `:540-563`, `:716-730`.

## Matching

- The index ships with the library and the match runs on the device; nothing about the singer or the song leaves it. The match runs once, when Lyrics is filled by a file, a scan, or a paste while Poem is empty; never on typing, and never when a stored song is opened (default 3: 'no existing seating moves'; a poem change re-tests every hand word). An existing song may be offered a poem in a later build; it is never filled.
- An index of incipits, titles, and word n-grams over the normalized texts, built at data-build time.
- The query is the scan's Lyrics as read, garbles and all, so the match must tolerate the reader's errors: score by the share of long exact words matched plus letter n-gram overlap, which item 9's alignment already leans on.
- The confidence threshold is measured, not guessed: on the build songs, the true poem must win by a margin, and a song not in the library must find nothing above the threshold. The held-out set includes two Russian translations of one source poem (Heine and Goethe in Lermontov, Fet, Tyutchev, Maikov, Mikhailov) and three songs not in the library. The margin is between the top candidate and the second. The match score and the alignment's anchors are one instrument, so a wrong poem yields no anchored stretch and tier 1 cannot fire inside it. The build songs set the threshold; row 114 is measured against it and never tunes it.
- A song whose Lyrics is too short or too garbled to match (a page that read as almost nothing) gets no fill and no false offer.

## The reader-divergence loop (desk-side, not in the app)

On the 18 build songs the truth files are the labels (Dann's engravings), not the poem; a poem adds nothing there. The loop's purpose is songs with no truth file (the IMSLP set, QUEUE row 82's history), where a library poem is the only label. A training line is taken only where the alignment is anchored by exact long-word matches on both sides of the row and the row's syllable count equals the poem segment's; a row where the composer departed from the poem is not a label. Confusion evidence is collected the same way. No model, wordlist, or DAWG built for the reader may include a poem of any gate song: the library at runtime may carry row 114's poems, the training corpus may not, and the threshold is set without them. Both files of row 114 stay out of every training set; the second file stays sealed.

## Addendum, 2026-11-03 02:2x: the library and Piece share one register of people (Dann's direction, not yet reviewed)

Dann, 02:16: *"We already provide dropdown selection lists in Piece. this is to standardize spelling and male the user's interations with Ilya easier. Maybe we can integrate Poem with Piece somwhow, like cross-referencing the authors and composers as we form our Poem corpus?"* (transcribed as written).

Read this session on `origin/Shane` `8f6c3528`: `apps/web/src/lib/composers-poets.ts` holds `COMPOSERS` (25) and `POETS` (37), each with Latin "Surname, Given", Cyrillic, dates as a string (`'1809–1842'`), and a French display form (N.78). The song record carries title, composer, poet, translator, opus, transcriber (`library/types.ts:266`). `metadata-provenance.ts:11-14` fills blank fields only from a score header and keeps whatever the singer typed.

1. **One register.** Every library entry names its poet, translator if any, and the composers who set it by the same keys as `COMPOSERS` and `POETS`. A person the library needs and the register lacks (Grekov) is added to the register, so the dropdown, the paper, and the library spell one name one way. The register's dates become structured (birth and death years) so the public-domain status is computed from the same record the dropdown shows.
2. **Piece narrows the match.** When the singer has chosen a composer or poet in Piece, the match searches that person's poems first: a stronger, cheaper signal than a garbled scan. It is a hint, never a filter: a confident match outside the chosen person is still found and shown, because a wrong Piece choice must not force a wrong poem.
3. **The match fills Piece.** On a confident match, Piece's empty fields (title, poet, translator, composer) fill from the library entry, under the same rule as a score header: blank fields only, a "from library" tag that fades on the first hand edit, the singer's own entries never overwritten (`metadata-provenance.ts`, §A.6).
4. **The register drives the corpus.** For each composer in the register, the library records which of his songs it holds and which are missing, so curation works through a checklist, not a guess.
5. **Translations.** The translator field already exists; Kabalevsky op. 52 carries Shakespeare as poet and Marshak as translator, and the public-domain test applies to the translator (change 2).
