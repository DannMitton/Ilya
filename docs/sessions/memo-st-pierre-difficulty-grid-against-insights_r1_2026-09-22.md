## Her instrument, in one paragraph

**Twenty-five challenging elements in five categories:** melody, rhythm, harmony, text,
piano accompaniment. Each element is scored **minimal (1), moderate (2), extensive (3)**
according to the degree of its occurrence in the score. **Elements not present are not
assessed.** Column subtotals are multiplied by their point values and summed, and the total
gives **elementary, intermediate or advanced** (Tables 3 and 4, her pp. 73).

**She built it because nothing existed.** Her literature review covered **forty-two guides**
(journals and syllabi) and concluded that *"no acknowledged standard exists for assessing
the level of difficulty of compositions for solo voice and piano"* (her p. 62). It draws on
Carol Kimball's components of style, J. Arden Hopkin's seven values, Christopher Arneson's
sample rubrics, and her own teaching.

## THREE THINGS THAT BEAR ON ILYA

### 1. She reached Ilya's three rows independently, and for a different purpose

**Her melody category leads with wide range, then high or low tessitura, and she defines
tessitura BY THE PASSAGGI** (her p. 74): a high tessitura lies mostly around the secondo
passaggio and above for women's voices and above the primo for men's; a low tessitura lies
around the primo and below for women, and in the lower chest range for men.

**Range, passaggi, tessitura. That is exactly Insights' three rows** (`insights.ts`,
`fit.range`, `fit.crossings`, `fit.tessitura`). **She got there from repertoire pedagogy;
Ilya got there from measurement.** That convergence is worth more than either alone.

### 2. She scores DEGREE. Ilya reports DIRECTION only

**Hers:** a wide range is minimal just above an octave and a half, moderate at about two
octaves, extensive beyond two.

**Ilya's:** the flag column says `Contained`, `Above`, `Below`, `Wider`, `No threshold`
(`i18n.ts:1479-1483`). **`Wider` does not say how much wider.**

**This is the same instinct Dann followed on 2026-09-22** when he replaced a bare ordering
with "by how much time you spend singing it". **A candidate improvement to Insights, not
specified and not numbered.**

### 3. A second source for the crossing finding, which Ilya does not cite

She quotes **John Nix**: *"Pieces that have high notes on the low first formant vowels /i/,
/ɪ/, /u/, /ʊ/, and /e/ . . . require significant modification in the upper octave of the
female voice"*, and that *"voiced consonants can be quite problematic on high pitches"*
(her p. 74, citing Nix).

**That is `insights.finding.crossing` almost exactly**: the vowel meets the first resonance,
the tone wants to turn. **Ilya currently frames this from Bozeman.** Nix names the specific
vowels. **Whether to cite him is Dann's, and the full Nix reference is in her footnotes,
which the desk has not opened.**

## WHAT DOES NOT TRANSFER

- **Harmony, text and piano accompaniment** assess a pianist's and a reader's difficulty.
  **Ilya does not read the piano** and is not grading a text's maturity or dialect.
- **Her single summed grade is a declaration.** `PRODUCT.md` requires Insights to forecast
  and never declare, so a piece labelled "advanced" is the shape of claim this page refuses.
- **Different questions.** Hers asks whether a piece suits a singer's stage of training.
  **Ilya asks whether it suits a specific measured voice.** Her tessitura element is already
  voice-relative through the passaggi, which is the point of contact.
