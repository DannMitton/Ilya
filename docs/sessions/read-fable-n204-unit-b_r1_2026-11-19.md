## The paragraph a developer needs

### The parsers

`MusicXmlScoreParser` (`musicxml-parser.ts`) and `MnxScoreParser` (`mnx-parser.ts`) each turn one file into one `ParsedScore`. Both pick the first part that carries lyrics as the vocal part, walk it bar by bar with an exact-fraction cursor, and give every note and rest the id `m{measureIndex}-{numerator}-{denominator}` built from that cursor. Both store one primary syllable per note (the lowest verse present) and put the other verses in `versesInfo`. Neither checks the text: what the file says under a note is what the score holds. The repair of that text happens later and outside this package, in `apps/web/src/lib/score/ingestion/ingest.ts`, which runs `foldDictionMarks` and then `foldVowelessSyllables` on every arrival. A score taken straight from `parse()` is therefore not yet under ONE VOWEL PER NOTE. The MusicXML parser has had three pieces moved out to stay under its line ceiling (`notations.ts`, `grace.ts`, `barline-style.ts`); the MNX parser has not, and it reads less: no grace notes, no slurs, no tempo words, no fermata on a rest. Both call `adoptPickupMeter` (`pickup.ts`) so a pickup written as its own metre is read one way. The two files copy their shared helpers by hand; the MusicXML header says so and defers the fix to this audit.

### The analysis files

Everything after the parse is a pure function of a `ParsedScore`. Three layers. First, projections that return a changed copy and keep ids: `scoreInPerformanceOrder` (repeats and jumps taken, through `unfold.ts`), `verseView` (one verse's words), `shiftVocalOctave`, `transposeScore`, and `engraveInKey`. Second, the forecast: `analyzeScore` (`overlay-engine.ts`) asks the caller's vowel resolver for one vowel per note and writes one `AnalyzedEvent` per note, keyed by event id; `noteConditions` (`conditions.ts`) adds per-note facts for Insights. Third, the measurement layer: `aggregatePhonation` (`phonation.ts`) sums sounding time per pitch and per vowel in quavers and lets each bar's metre choose between two readings of a note's length; `pachecoTessitura` cuts a band from those sums; `resolveTempo` (`tempo-seam.ts`) answers "what tempo" by four rungs (singer, metronome mark, tempo word through `tempo-lexicon.ts` and `tempo-terms.ts`, then an estimate from the metre). The vowel is never decided here; the app passes a resolver in. Two things a newcomer will trip on: a performance-order score repeats events with the same id and the same `measureIndex`, so any per-bar sum over it counts a repeated bar twice; and "is this note held" is computed two ways (`isLongSustain` in `sustain.ts` reads metronome marks only; `noteConditions` reads the tempo seam over a whole tie chain).

### `types.ts`

`types.ts` is the contract: `ParsedScore`, `Measure`, `VocalLineEvent`, `SyllableInfo`, and the parser interface with its warning and error codes. Read `VocalLineEvent` and `SyllableInfo` first; the rest hangs off them. Three conventions carry the weight and none is enforced by the type: a note with no `syllable` is a continuation (melisma), so anything that removes a syllable makes a note look like one; `SyllableInfo`'s top-level `text` is the lowest verse present on that note, not always verse 1, and `versesInfo` is the full record when more than one verse sings; `duration.fraction` is called derived, yet the MusicXML parser fills it from `<duration>` and the display fields from `<type>`, so the two can disagree and `phonation.ts` exists to arbitrate. Several fields are set by the app and never by a parser (`singerTempo`, `dictionMarks`, the warning codes `words-partly-read` and `extender-notes`). The file is 999 lines against a 1,000-line cap for new files, and its three newest fields are squeezed into trailing one-line comments for that reason.

**B1. `MusicXmlScoreParser.readNote` with `readLyricSegments`: two vowels are written under one note and accepted.** A `<lyric>` holding an `<elision>` is joined with U+203F into one `text` («ко‿я»), stored with `segments` and `parseFlag: 'elided'`, and reported under the generic code `unrecognised-element`. `musicxml-parser.test.ts` pins this as intended ("splits an elided syllable pair into segments and flags it"). 0.1 says more than one vowel under a note is an alert and is never accepted as a reading. On the file path I found no check for the many-vowel half: `ingest.ts` runs `foldDictionMarks` and `foldVowelessSyllables` only, and `vowelsPerNote` is imported by `omr/underlay-page.ts` and `text/poem-join.ts` alone (grep). Proposed: a warning code of its own for this case so the seam can count it; the `segments` doc reworded to state the rule; the many-vowel check added at the ingest seam (outside this unit, named for the desk). Risk: `segments` and `parseFlag` have one non-test reader (`vowelless-fold.ts`, which clears them). Proof: a new case in the app's `fixture-rule.test.ts` over an elided fixture.

**B2. `splitElision` (`mnx-parser.ts`): the same acceptance, by heuristic, with no warning.** A token holding U+203F or an ordinary space (U+0020; byte-checked) is kept whole under one note and flagged `elided`, segments typed `whole`. ## Behaviour defects met during the read

Outside the tiers. Each changes, or can change, what a singer sees. None is fixed here.

- **D1. A dynamic or an expression word is read as a tempo (ran).** `resolveTempoTerm` allows an edit distance of 2 against every key, and the keys include three-to-five-letter words (`vif`, `lent`, `vite`, `slow`, `fast`, `allo`). Ran against the file: "dim.", "mf", "f", "ff", "sf", "sim.", "div.", and "V.S." resolve to *vif* (fast, 132); "ten." and "legato" to *lento* (52); "Fine" to *allegro*. `MusicXmlScoreParser.parse` pushes the words of every `<direction>` into `tempoWords`, and rung 3 of `resolveTempo` takes the first that resolves. Ran through `resolveTempo`: words `["dolce", "dim."]` give `inferred`, *vif*, 132, printed text "dim."; `["espressivo", "mf"]` the same; `["cantabile", "Fine"]` gives *allegro*. A song with no metronome mark and no recognised tempo word is then told its tempo was inferred from "dim.". The lexicon's header puts expression marks outside what any tempo lexicon resolves; the fuzzy pass resolves them.
- **D2. `shiftVocalOctave` leaves grace notes at the written octave.** It returns `{ ...parsed, clefs: [], vocalLine }` with shifted pitches; `graceNotes` rides through untouched, where `transposeScore` and `engraveInKey` both move it. `InsightsPane.svelte` and `MarkupPane.svelte` pass the result on as `printedReading`. Whether a later layer corrects the grace pitches is NOT ESTABLISHED.
- **D3. A repeated bar reads as untrusted in performance order (ran).** `aggregatePhonation` sums each bar by `measureIndex` against the metre once; `scoreInPerformanceOrder` repeats a bar's events with the same index. Ran: one 2/4 bar holding a triplet whose `fraction` omits the tuplet (the `musx2mxl` case the header describes), with a backward repeat: notated, the verdict is `metre-chose-notation` and no bar is untrusted; in performance order the verdict is `untrusted`, one bar, 8 quavers. `aggregatePhonation`'s doc invites a performance-order score. Which score the app's six call sites pass is NOT ESTABLISHED.
- **D4. `feltBeat` returns the whole-note glyph for a half-note beat (ran for the code point).** For a metre over 2 the glyph is U+1D15D; `GLYPH_OF.half` in the same file is U+1D15E; for a metre over 1 it returns U+1D15D twice. The app draws its own glyph (`tempo/beat-glyph.ts`), and I found no reader of `.beatGlyph` (grep), so the field may be dead as well as wrong.
- **D5. Two notes can share an id in MusicXML (read, not run).** `readNote` builds the id from the bar and the cursor alone; `<backup>` rewinds the cursor; no voice or staff filter exists. A second voice, or a second staff in the part that carries the lyrics, yields a second event at the same position with the same id, and `tieFlags` (keyed by id) is overwritten. The header's invariant "No two events share an id, under any input" does not hold for generated ids. Stored corrections key on the id. The one test that uses `<backup>` holds a chord tone only.
- **D6. The sustained-ceiling gate sees metronome marks and single events only.** `analyzeScore` calls `isLongSustain(ev, parsed.tempoMarkings)`. A note held through a tie, a song whose tempo is a word, the singer's own tempo, and the estimate never reach the 2.5-second test; only a fermata does. `noteConditions` documents this and builds a second test beside it. The `[o]→[ɑ]` cover forecast reads the first.
- **D7. A tied `continue` note points the other way in each parser.** In MusicXML's tie pass the middle note's `partnerEventId` ends as the next note; in MNX's it stays the previous note (`sourceEvent.tied ?? …`). One app module reads `partnerEventId` (grep).
- **D8. The same song says less as MNX.** See the drift table: no grace notes (N.187 says they are drawn), no slurs, no fermata on a rest, no tempo words. With no tempo words, a `.musx` song without a metronome mark always falls to the rung 4 estimate. Whether MNX can carry tempo words at all is NOT ESTABLISHED.
- **D9. `calibrationSnapshot` is not a full copy.** `deepCopyProfile` omits `fR1Source`, `fR2Source`, and `intake`. What the two app readers of `calibrationSnapshot` need is NOT ESTABLISHED.
- **D10. `assignWordContexts` walks primary syllables only.** On a note where verse 1 holds a melisma and verse 2 sings, the primary is verse 2's; verse 2's other syllables sit in `versesInfo` on neighbouring notes and are never visited, so its word is "interrupted" (a warning) and its `wordContext` is partial. An elided pair takes the first segment's role, so the second word's start is lost. `wordContext` has no non-test reader in the app (grep), so the visible effect is the warning count.
- **D11. The version warning does not match its words.** `parse` warns when the major version is under 3; the message says "predates 3.1"; the comment says "Newer than tested → warn". A 3.0 file gets no warning; a newer one gets none either.
- **D12. `TempoResolution.printedText` under the singer's tempo is the first direction word of any kind** (`words[0]?.text`), which can be "dolce".
- **D13. MNX `time.count` is not checked for a positive value**, where MusicXML requires `beats > 0`; a zero gives `expectedDuration` 0/n.
