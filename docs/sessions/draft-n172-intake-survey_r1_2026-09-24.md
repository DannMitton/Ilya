## Design principles and their sources

1. **Label every point in words.** Reliability is higher when all points are labelled than when only some are (Krosnick and Presser 2010, p. 274, read 2026-09-24).
2. **Five points.** Reliability is lower with two or three points and levels off after about seven (Krosnick and Presser 2010, p. 271). Five is the desk's compromise between reliability and a singer's patience, and it matches Dreyfus's five stages, so point n in an area is stage n in that area. DESK CHOICE.
3. **Ask about the thing itself, never "agree or disagree".** Construct-specific scales are more valid and reliable than agree/disagree formats (Krosnick and Presser 2010, pp. 276-277).
4. **One thing per question, in simple, concrete words** (Krosnick and Presser 2010, p. 264).
5. **No "not sure" option.** Such options do not improve data quality and attract satisficing (Krosnick and Presser 2010, pp. 281-283). Every question can be skipped instead.
6. **Anchor each point in what the singer can do, not in a judgement of worth.** This is the idea behind behaviourally anchored rating scales (Smith and Kendall 1963; not read, named from search results), and it is the same move Dann's own outline makes with Benner (1984) and the ACGME milestones' narrative anchors (`~/Documents/Informed Ear Article/Outline_Informed_Ear_v7_JOS.docx` §2.3.5). It also keeps rule 9: the words equip and never alarm.
7. **Stages per area, not one global label.** Dann's outline §6.3.1: one person can be at different stages in different strata. So the area answers carry the Dreyfus stages, and the global "Where are you in your singing?" question is dropped. DESK PROPOSAL.
8. **Parallel wording for points 3 to 5 across every question**, so a singer learns the scale once.

## The questions

Each: five labelled points, optional, editable at any time; with no answer, Ilya assumes point 3.

**1. The top of your voice, around and above your upper passaggio.**
1. I rarely sing there yet.
2. I can reach it, but it feels unpredictable.
3. It works in practice when I prepare it.
4. It is reliable in performance most of the time.
5. It is reliable, and I can colour it as I choose.

**2. The bottom of your range.**
1. I rarely sing there yet.
2. The notes sound, but they lack body.
3. They carry when I prepare them.
4. They carry in performance most of the time.
5. They carry reliably, and I can colour them as I choose.

**3. Moving through your passaggio.**
1. I notice a clear shift in my sound there.
2. I can smooth it in exercises, not yet in songs.
3. It is smooth in songs when I prepare it.
4. It is smooth in performance most of the time.
5. It is seamless, and I can choose where the colour changes.

**4. Long held notes.**
1. Long notes tire me quickly.
2. I can hold them, but the tone wavers or thins.
3. They stay steady when I plan the breath.
4. They are steady in performance most of the time.
5. They are steady, and I can shape them as I choose.

**5. Singing softly up high.**
1. Soft singing up high is not available to me yet.
2. I can sing softly there, but it feels fragile.
3. It works when I prepare it.
4. It is reliable in performance most of the time.
5. It is reliable, and I can move freely between soft and loud there.

**6. What you would like from Insights.** One switch per topic, all on by default: high notes; low notes; moving through the passaggio; held notes; soft singing up high; things to notice. Shown beside questions 1 to 5 in one panel, per the desk's critique of 23:17.

## How the answers act (from the curation draft, rule 7)

An answer moves only the "how hard is this for this singer" half of stakes, in its own area. It never hides the fit verdict or a "notice this" line. A switched-off topic is counted quietly ("2 observations hidden by your settings"), never lost without the singer knowing.
