## What the survey is, and its limits for us

- **A survey of the field up to 2011,** by a group whose own work is on staff detection and symbol classification. It is a secondary source: 16 of the 36 rows are claims it attributes to other papers (`claim_type` = `cited`). **Where the plan leans on one of those, the primary must be read before it is cited.**
- **Its centre of gravity is handwritten music.** It says commercial products did "quite good" on printed music (p. 174) and spends its open issues on manuscripts.
- **It predates the learned, end-to-end readers** that the project's July 2026 landscape scan covers. It describes the classical tradition, which is the tradition Ilya's reader belongs to.
- **It gives lyrics one paragraph** (p. 182) and says nothing about dynamics text, tempo words, or Cyrillic.

## What it tells us, in the order of the plan

**The shape of the pipeline is the field's own (rows 002, 004, 021).** Four stages: clean the image; recognize the symbols; reconstruct the music from them; write it out. Ours adds one thing the survey names only in passing: an explicit structure, the page model, held between reconstruction and output.

**The staff is the ruler (rows 003, 011, 012, 013).** Line thickness and staff spacing are measured first and everything is sized from them, so that nothing depends on fixed numbers (p. 178). The survey warns that fixed thresholds make a system unable to adapt, which is the audit's error 7 said by the field. Ilya's own staff-space estimate is already the robust form the survey credits to Cardoso and Rebelo 2010 (`reader.py:250-251`).

**Dann's design-software tools are the field's standard list (rows 003, 008, 009, 010).** Enhancement, binarization, noise removal, blurring, de-skewing, and morphological operations (p. 177). Two cautions come with them: binarization introduces artifacts of its own (p. 177), and the one content-aware method known to the authors sized its cleaning from the staff (Pinto et al. 2011, p. 178).

**Tilted and bent staves are a known problem in printed music, and the projection method is the known weak one (rows 015, 016, 017, 018, 019).** The simplest staff finder assumes straight horizontal lines and tries whole-page rotations (p. 180). That is the reader's current method and the desk's failed rotation test. Building staves from short segments linked across the page has precedents (Miyao and Okamoto 2004; Szwoch 2005; Dutta et al. 2010; Dalitz et al. 2008). So does a second route we have not tried: the staff line as the shortest dark path from the left margin to the right (Cardoso et al. 2009). **That belongs in the plan as the named pivot if the strip trace misses its gate.** The survey also says plainly that curvature and breaks were inadequately solved (p. 180).

**Erasing staff lines is a trade (row 014).** It simplifies what follows and it breaks symbols. Some systems never erase. This supports keeping the untouched scan underneath and working on purpose-made copies.

**"Unsure" has a name in the field: the reject option (row 024).** A classifier that carries a confidence measure and declines to name a broken or touching symbol, leaving it for a person (p. 182).

**Checks that decide, not only flag (rows 005, 022, 028, 029, 035).** Reconstruction uses rules of notation "to validate and solve ambiguities" from recognition (p. 175, paraphrased). Rossant and Bloch 2007 keep several hypotheses per symbol and decide by consistency, then check the beats in each bar (pp. 181, 183). Ng and colleagues use the key and time signatures to detect and correct errors (p. 183). Rebelo et al. 2011 propose asking whether each bar's durations add up to the time signature (p. 187). **This is direct support for the plan's third departure, bar arithmetic as evidence.** One caution: the one architecture with full feedback between stages had no practical results (p. 184).

**The singer in the loop is the survey's own recommendation (row 036).** For hard material it calls interactive OMR "a realistic and practical solution" (p. 187), and it points to systems that learn from an expert user's corrections. That is the one-tap fix, and it suggests keeping each of the singer's corrections as a labelled example.

**Measuring is harder than it looks (rows 007, 032, 033, 034).** Vendor accuracy figures of about 90 percent are called unreliable because the test scores and the method are not stated (p. 185). No study had measured how results depend on scan resolution (p. 177), which fits our finding that one page gave 9, 4, and 2 staves on three rasters. Comparing MusicXML files is ambiguous because one score has many correct encodings (p. 185), so our scorer must compare musical facts.

**Degraded photocopies are where the field reports failure (rows 018, 031).** Most systems "fail when the input image is highly degraded such as photocopies" (p. 184, paraphrased closely). The hardening phase should be planned with that on the table.

**Lyrics (row 025).** Thin. A lyric line is found as a baseline, and it may curve; lyrics should be tied to notes. Both sources worked on other repertoire.

## What it does not give us

Nothing on reading dynamics or tempo text. Nothing on any non-Latin script. No measured accuracy for any printed-music system that we could use as a ceiling. Nothing after 2011.

## The primaries worth reading next, in order

1. Rossant and Bloch 2007 (hypotheses, consistency, bar check, printed scores).
2. Cardoso et al. 2009 (stable paths) and Dalitz et al. 2008 (staff-removal comparison, with deformed test scores).
3. Rebelo et al. 2011 (extraction guided by musical rules).
4. Burgoyne et al. 2009 (lyric line extraction).
5. Bellini, Bruno, and Nesi 2007 (how to score an OMR system).

## On the extraction table's form

**Kept from the Insights tables:** one claim per row; a page on every row; the same `claim_type` vocabulary, with `cited` for anything the authors attribute to someone else; `source_file` in the library's naming; `citekey`.

**Changed, with reasons:**

1. **`read_by` is new.** It records who read the page, with what instrument, and when. The Insights tables cannot show whether a row was read on the page or carried over from a summary, and `ENVIRONMENT.md` records two failures of exactly that kind.
2. **`attributed_to` is new.** For a survey, most claims belong to someone else, and the row should name whom. The Insights tables put this in `notes`, where it cannot be sorted or counted.
3. **`plan_bearing` replaces the voice columns** (`vowels`, `pitch_range`, `voice_types`, `map_bands`), which do not apply here.

**Suggested for the Insights tables too:** the three batches the desk opened carry 17, 19, and 21 columns. One fixed set of columns, with the optional ones left blank, would let the batches be merged and counted without a conversion step.

**Quotations.** The table carries one, of five words. Every other row is a paraphrase with its page, so a reader verifies it on the page in seconds.
