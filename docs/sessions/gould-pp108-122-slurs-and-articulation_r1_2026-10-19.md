## Slurs

1. **p. 109, "Slurs".** Besides marking a phrase, the slur means for the singer the notes sung to one syllable (she points to "Syllabic slurs", p. 435, NOT READ).
2. **p. 109, "Design".** The slur is the tie's tapered arc. With different outer pitches its ends sit at different heights and it tilts between the first and last note; aim for a consistent curve. A long slur is flattened, even flat in the middle, to stay close to the stave.
3. **p. 110.** On a stave, the arc sits in a stave-space, to be most conspicuous.
4. **p. 110, "Positioning relative to the stave".** When all stems within the slur point one way, the slur usually goes between the outer noteheads (the notehead side). With mixed stem directions, above the stave, except where a beam may be in the way. In cramped conditions a long slur may go above the stave whatever the stems, to be clear of dynamics. Take all the notes into account; do not swap sides between systems (p. 112).
5. **pp. 110 to 111, "Slurs next to noteheads".** The ends may sit as close as half a stave-space from the centre of a notehead; where ties sit at the notehead centre, the slur moves further out so its ends can centre on the notehead too.
6. **p. 111, "Slurs at stem end".** Where nothing is obstructed, the slur may come slightly closer to the noteheads, beside the stems. With staccato or tenuto marks inside, it stays at the stem ends. With outer notes of opposite stem direction, the stem-end of the slur moves toward the noteheads so the slur does not tilt against the pitches. It must not come too close if there is room: **"It should always remain outside a beam"** (her example: two beamed notes, the slur over the beam, "and not" between the beam and the heads). This is song 1 bar 10's first slur on the alias, 2026-10-19 21:4x.
7. **p. 112, "Slurs over a system break".** The slur stops just short of the last barline, and restarts after the clef, key, and time signature but before any accidental; each half looks clearly open-ended and is angled toward the next pitch, which tells an open slur from an open tie.
8. **pp. 112 to 113, "Slur length".** One slur covers the whole phrase. Her example on p. 113 draws a long slur over a passage with triplets, passing outside the triplet's numeral (read from the picture; the text states no slur-and-tuplet rule in these pages).
9. **p. 113, "Slurs within slurs".** The shorter slur (articulation) goes closest to the notes, the longer (phrase) outside it.
10. **p. 114.** A phrase with rests may take one solid slur. Notes on one stem take one slur. (Grace-note slurs: p. 129, read for N.187.)

## Articulation marks

11. **p. 117, "Placing articulation next to noteheads or stems".** Articulation goes to the notehead, where it is most conspicuous; never below down-stems except in double-stemmed writing. The strong accent (^) usually goes above the stave whatever the stems. **When space below the stave is limited, all articulation may go above.**
12. **p. 118.** Articulation is centred in line with the notehead, beside the head or the stem; staccato dots and wedges alone may centre on the stem.
13. **p. 119.** Place stemless notes' marks as if stemmed (a centre-line note as if down-stemmed). Keep a consistent distance from each head. On the stave, no closer than the first clear stave-space; tenuto lines and staccato dots centred in a space.
14. **p. 120.** A stave-line never obscures a mark. Accents (> and ^) and wedges are usually best outside the stave; they may come onto it when they would otherwise be far from the note; > is clearest centred in a space. A second mark takes the next stave-space out; never two marks in one space.
15. **p. 121, "Distance from stems".** On the stave, a mark goes in the first clear space beyond the stem end; outside the stave, about half a stave-space from the stem.
16. **p. 121, "Placing slurs with other articulation marks".** The smallest signs go closest to the notehead. Usually only tenuto lines and staccato marks go inside the first and last notes of a slur.
17. **p. 122.** Marks in the middle of a slur go inside it. **Accents at the beginning and end of a slur usually go outside it, so the slur stays closer to the noteheads**, unless that would leave them too far from the note. With mixed stem directions under a slur above the stave, marks still go beside each notehead. A slur is centred on a stem; with a tenuto between slur and stem, on the notehead.
