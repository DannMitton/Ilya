## 3. Enhancements, ranked by what a singer gains

3. **Let the singer state the mode once, and never ask for it.** The file may not say major or minor; the singer and the teacher know at a glance. ## 4. The naming question, section 3c

**My reading: where the file states no mode, Ilya names the move by its interval and the landing by its signature, stated outright in the words the key signature card already uses. Never a tonic, and never arithmetic on signatures.**

What singers and teachers say to each other when a song moves is the interval and the voice: "take it down a tone", "the low key", "the medium-voice edition". They name a tonic to identify an edition ("the one in F minor"), and when the file does not say, they read it off the page in a second. Nobody says "two sharps fewer". That half of form 3 also fails as soon as a move crosses from sharps to flats: two sharps to three flats has no sentence of that kind a musician would say.

Form 2, "C major or A minor", is true for tonal music and asserts a major-or-minor frame on a modal song, which this repertoire has. It doubles every readout and cannot fit on a stop.

Form 1 is closest, and it needs the interval beside it. The singer reads the line while looking at the staves, where the new signature is already drawn; the interval is the part the staves cannot show. The signature still earns its place for one reason the plan did not weigh: **it is the only thing that tells twins apart when there is no tonic.** From a print in two sharps, "Down a minor third" lands on five sharps and on seven flats (`transposition.ts:275-278`; `apps/web/src/lib/melody/transposition-ruler.test.ts:130`). INSIGHTS can say the interval alone, and already does when it has no key name (`apps/web/src/lib/insights/key-lines.ts:153`); the ruler cannot, because it offers both twins side by side.

The form, place by place, for a score with no stated mode:

| place | mode stated (unchanged) | no mode stated |
|---|---|---|
| stop face | C | 0 |
| stop face, twins | B and C♭ | 5♯ and 7♭ |
| readout, away from print | Down a whole tone · C major | Down a whole tone · no sharps or flats |
| readout, on the printed stop | D major | 2 sharps |
| dock chips | As printed · D | As printed · 2♯ |
| KEY row | D major, printed | 2 sharps, printed |
| page header | Transposed down a whole tone from D major, at the singer's choice. | Transposed down a whole tone from the printed key, at the singer's choice. |
| INSIGHTS line | Down a whole tone · C major | Down a whole tone. (as built today) |

The words for the signature exist in both languages and a singer who has corrected a key has already read them: "No sharps or flats", "1 sharp", "%s sharps", "1 flat", "%s flats" (`i18n.ts:582-586`). One term per concept holds.

**What I am unsure of, and it is taste.** On stop faces, counts do not read as a scale the way tonic letters do; position and the readout carry the distance. The runner-up is the pair as a circle-of-fifths chart prints it, "C/a", which reads as a scale and names the signature by convention without choosing a mode. It costs width on every stop, most on the phone dock, and it has no settled French form. The face for an empty signature is "0" in my table; "♮" is the alternative. Tether 18 applies: the desk draws both face treatments at desktop and dock width and Dann rules from the drawing.

**French, as marked drafts, his to rule.** DRAFT header: « Transposition {interval} à partir de la tonalité imprimée, au choix de l'interprète. », built from `key.page.header` (`i18n.ts:2370`) and « Tonalité imprimée » (`:2365`); agreement checked against « tonalité », feminine. DRAFT readout: « Un ton plus bas · aucune altération », from `key.ruler.down` (`:2375`) and `correct.keyNone` (`:582`). Open choices that are his: (a) the row's tag for a score with no mode, « TONALITÉ » to match the ruler's strings or « ARMURE », which the tree already uses for key signature (`:575-579`) and is the exact word for what the row then shows; (b) the row's word for "printed" agrees with a feminine noun here, « imprimée », where the tempo row has « imprimé » (`:1914`); (c) whether "no sharps or flats" takes a lower-case form in mid-line, since the existing string opens with a capital. The stop faces are glyphs and need no French.
