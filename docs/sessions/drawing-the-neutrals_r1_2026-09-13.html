2. #3A352F, the stave’s furniturename it, do not merge it37 uses, no token, 0.129 from --ink-primary

The five lines and the barlines are #3A352F. The noteheads and stems are --ink-primary.

This is not a duplicate to be swept up. The census found the split held across all 29 renderer sites: #3A352F is the furniture a singer reads through (lines, clefs, key signatures, barlines, rests, ledger lines, multi-bar numerals), and --ink-primary is what a singer sings (noteheads, stems, beams, accidentals, ties, lyrics). It is the most consistent rule in the neutrals and it has no name, which is why it looks like a stray.

4. --stone-300 and --desk-surfacekeep both0.001 apart in L, and two different jobs

--stone-300: 26 hairlines, and this

Bar numbers

An off switch. The track is the only thing that says off.

--desk-surface: the ground, under everything

A page, floating on the desk.

They are one thousandth of a lightness step apart and they will look identical side by side. They are still two ideas: one is the chrome a control is drawn from, the other is the ground the whole app sits on. Merging them means that the day you warm the desk, every hairline and every off switch warms with it. Recommended: leave them as two, and let them happen to be near.

--paper-light is meant to be the one visual difference between the reading aid and the page. The census measured that difference at 0.018, which is under one just-noticeable difference: the fill that is supposed to separate them does not separate them. That is a design question about what the reading aid should look like, not a question about how many neutrals there are, so it belongs with the drawer work rather than inside this ruling.
