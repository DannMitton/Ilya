## The balance rule (ruled in 00:03)

A label enters the shortlist when it is in common use in at least two of the five traditions, or central to Russian repertoire, or a label singers commonly use for themselves. Everything else is free text, shown as typed. The singer's label is displayed; its parent routes the sources and the Bozeman band. Kloiber's and Boldrey's ranges are ranges of roles for casting and are never used as limits for the resonance check.

## What Boldrey says that bears on the design

- **Categories are for repertoire, and they are imposed.** Boldrey and Caldwell, p. 6: voice categories "have one clear and simple purpose: to make it easy to match voices with repertoire"; "the terms are imposed and arbitrary, merely labels"; "most singers easily fit into two or even three neighboring categories."
- **The singer owns the label.** p. 13: "voice categories can never fully describe your voice"; "The categorization of your voice is a personal matter, something that can only come from your journey of self-discovery, and ultimately is in your hands alone." This is the published ground for Dann's dignity principle (2026-09-29 23:42).
- **Neighbours.** p. 16, footnote: neighbouring categories are "those one (two for sopranos) above or below."
- **Voices move.** p. 9: Bergonzi went baritone, leggero tenor, lyric tenor, lirico-spinto; Sutherland lyric coloratura to dramatic coloratura to dramatic soprano.
- **Parent ranges.** p. 11 gives a "general range" for each parent (soprano b♭ to c''', extended g to f'''; mezzo g to b♭''; contralto f to f''; tenor c to c''; baritone c to f', extended G to a'; bass-baritone A♭ to f', extended E to g'; bass F to e', extended E♭ to f'). pp. 17 to 18 give a "Normal Range" per subcategory. Helmholtz notation: c' is middle C.
- **Countertenor, in his words** (p. 26): "a male voice singing in a female register, though not in falsetto"; the soprano countertenor has "the range of a modest mezzo-soprano", the alto countertenor "the ranges of a normal contralto." This differs from Dann's understanding (most countertenors are baritones with excellent falsetto, 00:01). Recorded, not ruled.
- **Soubrette is a character type** (p. 20): she "may be a soprano, a mezzo-soprano, or even a contralto."

## Where it lives in the tree (read by the desk 2026-09-30 at `47a6f8f`)

- **Storage exists, and nothing writes it.** `voice/profileStore.ts:81-88` holds an optional `voiceType` ("a routing key to Bozeman value-sets"); `engine/types.ts:35` types it as `string`. No component or route passes a value: `grep voiceType` in `routes/` and `lib/components/` returns nothing, and `CalibrationWizard.svelte:207` defaults it to `undefined`. So no stored voice carries one, and no migration is needed.
- **The intake home:** the wizard's Voice characteristics phase, `CalibrationWizard.svelte:1512` onward, which already asks for range (`:1526`), tessitura (`:1546`), and passaggi. Voice type goes first in that phase, because the courtesy check compares the other fields against it. Every field there is optional and saves on change (`:1515-1516`).
- **The print home:** `insights/InsightsPane.svelte:242-246` builds the identity line, `insights.identity` = "Insights for {voice} · calibrated {date}" (`i18n.ts:1471`, French `:1471`, uncalibrated `:1472`). It feeds the title header (`:599`) and the running head (`:492`). The label goes after the voice name: "Insights for Dann · basso cantante · calibrated 2026-09-12".
- **Routing:** `engine/plausibility.ts` buckets are soprano, tenor-mezzo, baritone, bass, union. DESK DEFAULT mapping: soprano → soprano; mezzo-soprano, contralto, tenor → tenor-mezzo; baritone → baritone; bass-baritone, bass → bass; countertenor and "Not sure" → union.
