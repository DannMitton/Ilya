## 0. The corridor. Measured first, and NOT cut

Dann found this on the `63c2bb4` walk and it is not in the brief. On the phone
a strip of empty sits between the drawer's content and the pull, and every
horizontal rule in the drawer stops short of the right edge.

**The instruction was: measure the pull's rendered width, then cut the reserve
to that plus a margin, unless the measurement says the reserve is already
correct. The measurement says it is already correct, so nothing was cut.**

### The numbers

Chromium under real touch emulation, `hasTouch` and `isMobile` both set, which
is what makes `@media (pointer: coarse)` match. **The instrument's likeliest
lie was named before the run and ruled out**: under a fine pointer the pull's
`::before` takes no `content`, so the pull would measure 20 px and the reserve
would look 24 px too generous. Both pointers were run.

| what | coarse pointer (the phone) | fine pointer |
|---|---|---|
| `.drawer-lip` painted box | **20 × 76 px** | 20 × 76 px |
| its `::before` touch extension | **44 × 88 px**, `right: 0` | `content: none`, absent |
| hit test: how deep the pull owns, at its own centre | **44 px** | 20 px |
| hit test: how deep the pull owns, anywhere else | **0 px** | 0 px |
| `.drawer-body` `padding-right` | **44 px** | 44 px |

Hit-tested with `document.elementFromPoint` at one-pixel steps inward from the
drawer's right edge, not read out of the stylesheet.

At 390 × 844 the pull's band is **y 384 to 460**. The drawer is 844 tall.

### Why the reserve is correct

The touch extension spans x 346 to 390. **A reserve of 24 px would put content
at x 366, and 20 px of it would sit under the pull.** 44 px is the smallest
reserve that leaves nothing under the pull, exactly.

### Where the tree disagrees with `STATE.md`, and the tree wins

`STATE.md` names the cause as "That reserve was set when the pull was wider.
Dann's ruling of 2026-08-19 made the pull a bare chevron and thinner, and
nobody re-measured the reserve."

**That is false against the tree.** The reserve has never measured the pull's
paint. It measures the pull's **touch target**, it was set to the target at
N.73 S1b, and the rule says so in its own words at
`apps/web/src/lib/components/Drawer/Drawer.svelte:1164-1178`: "the width of the
pull's TOUCH TARGET, which since N.73 S1b is wider than the 20px the pull
paints. The gutter measures the target, not the paint, so the pull can never
cover a control." S1b made the pull's paint narrower and its target wider in
the same ship. There is nothing stale to re-measure.

### What is actually true, and it is a finding rather than a fix

**The reserve costs 44 px of the drawer's full height to protect an 88 px
band.** The two rules Dann saw are the anchors' own `2px double` borders, and
at 390 × 844 they are drawn at y 303 and y 780 while the pull sits at y 384 to
460. Neither comes within 80 px of it.

**Exempting the pinned anchors from the reserve is NOT safe, and this was
measured rather than reasoned.** Every control in both anchors was enumerated
against the pull's band at five phone sizes:

| viewport | pull band | top anchor | anchor controls inside the band |
|---|---|---|---|
| 430 × 932 | 422–510 | 0–303 | none |
| 390 × 844 | 378–466 | 0–303 | none |
| 393 × 727 | 320–408 | 0–303 | none |
| 375 × 667 | 290–378 | 0–303 | none |
| 360 × 640 | **276–364** | 0–303 | **the NOTATION disclosure, y 245–289** |

At 360 × 640 the disclosure button falls inside the band. Exempt the top
anchor and its right 28 px goes under the pull, which is the exact thing the
ruled reserve exists to prevent.

**So the corridor stays, and closing it is a ruling rather than a
measurement.** Three shapes exist and none is this ship's to pick: accept it,
move the rules out of the reserve while the boxes stay inside it, or change
what the pull's target does on an open drawer.

**Dann's spine proposal was not built**, per `STATE.md`: the coordinating desk
argued against it and he did not overrule.

### 2.3 The `aria-controls` defect was bigger than the brief's account of it

Brief §3 says the inactive pair member points at an id that is not in the DOM.
**Both members did**, whenever the drawer was Learn's or Guide's, and the
deeper fault is that neither member has a panel at all.

`Drawer.svelte`'s scrolling box carried `role="tabpanel"`,
`id="tabpanel-{activeTab}"` and `aria-labelledby="tab-{activeTab}"` from the
four-tab shape, where each destination had its own drawer. **S2 ended that.**
The two pair members now share that box byte for byte, and a panel that does
not change when the tab changes is not that tab's panel.

**What was chosen, and it is a decision:** the drawer stops claiming to be a
tabpanel, and the pair members stop claiming to control one.

- `Drawer.svelte:235-254`: the three attributes are gone, and the comment there says why. The drawer is what
  its own outer element already declares, `<aside aria-label="Controls">`, a
  complementary landmark that stands beside the desk on every destination.
- `DeskHead.svelte:90-106`: `aria-controls` is gone, and the comment there says why. `role="tablist"`,
  `role="tab"` and `aria-selected` stay, and `aria-selected` is what a screen
  reader announces.

`aria-controls` is optional on a `tab` in ARIA 1.2. **An absent reference beats
a broken one.** Measured: `[role="tabpanel"]` count is now 0, and dangling
`aria-controls` is 0 on all seven migration arrivals and all four wall-closed
arrivals.

## 4. Decisions this brief did not rule, stated as decisions

1. **`activeTab` survives as a DERIVED value rather than being deleted.** Two
   consumers are still right to ask a four-way question: `HeaderBar` paints
   four hues for four working surfaces (Dann's ruling of 2026-08-19), and
   `DeskHead` draws four names. Neither is asking "where am I"; both are asking
   "which surface is this". `+page.svelte:205`. **One line to reverse.**
2. **`TAB_ORDER` stays four-way** (`+page.svelte:353`). It is used only for
   slide direction, and the direction it describes is the desk head's drawn
   order of four names. Splitting it would be splitting a thing that is not
   two things.
3. **`data-tab` STAYS** (`Drawer.svelte:219`). The brief asked. No selector and
   no script in the tree reads it, but it is the one mark on the drawer that
   tells Studio's two documents apart, which is exactly what a harness needs to
   prove S2's invariant. Ship one's walk used it for that, and §3.3 uses it
   again. Its cost is one prop.
4. **`Drawer` takes `destination` AND `activeTab`, two props where it had one.**
   `destination` drives every branch; `activeTab` drives `data-tab` and nothing
   else. Documented at `Drawer.svelte:11-21`, one doc comment each.
5. **The migration does not rewrite what it read.** A stored `not-a-tab` stays
   `not-a-tab` in `localStorage` and is re-mapped on every load. Rewriting it
   would be a new write at boot, and N.27 is open. Measured: the stored value
   is unchanged after every arrival in §3.1.
6. **`restoreSurface` and `surfaceFor` are pure functions in a plain `.ts`, so
   they are testable, and no test was written.** A test would move gate 4 off
   682, which needs Dann's permission, and the brief's done-when asks for
   baseline. **The migration is one `switch` away from a real unit test the day
   he wants one.**
7. **`Analysis` sits under the result summary rather than absorbing it.** §2.4.
8. **The drawer is not a tabpanel and the pair members control nothing.** §2.3.
   Two attributes deleted, none added.
9. **Print stays in the Clear-Print-Transcribe grid**, as the brief rules. The
   reason is now written beside it (`RootPanel.svelte:288-294`) instead of only
   in a brief.
10. **The `.env` edit in §3.1 is the only state changed on Dann's machine, and
    it was recorded before it was changed and restored after.**
