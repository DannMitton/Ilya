## A Walkthrough

*Deck line:* One complete session, from a scanned score to a printed sheet.

This walkthrough follows one song from start to finish: "В четырёх стенах" ("Within Four Walls"), the first song of Mussorgsky's cycle *Sunless*, from a scanned PDF of the score. You bring in the score, add the poem, name the piece, read the text word by word, check the notes Ilya read, measure your voice, read what Ilya says about the piece and your voice, and print. If you have only the poem, see "If you have only the poem" at the end.

### The interface at a glance

[SHOT 1: the whole screen on first load, drawer open on Input]

The drawer on the left holds the controls. The page on the right shows the result, and the page is what prints.

The drawer has three bands: **Piece**, for the song's name and your library; **Input**, for the score and the poem; and **Voice**, for your voice. Press a band to open or close it. On your first visit only Input is open.

Above the page are three tabs: **Text** shows the poem transcribed, word by word; **Markup** shows the score with your voice drawn on it; **Insights** says how this piece meets your voice. To their right, **Learn** opens a course in Russian lyric diction, and **Guide** opens this page.

The language pill at the top right switches everything between English and French. It always names the other language.

On a narrow screen the drawer slides over the page. The pull at the bottom edge reads **Drawer** when the page is showing and **Paper** when the drawer is up.

### Bringing in the score

[SHOT 2: the Input field with the PDF being dropped]

Drop the PDF on the Input field, or choose **choose a file** in the line under it. Ilya also takes a photograph of a page, MusicXML, and other score files in the same field. On a phone or tablet, the camera button beside the field takes the photograph for you.

Ilya reads the notes off the page. The first time in a session, the reader takes about a minute to start, then a few seconds for each page.

[SHOT 3: the card "Two things Ilya read from the page"]

Before it reads every page, Ilya asks you to confirm two things: the clef and the key signature. Check both against your own score, change either if it is wrong, and press **Read this page**.

When the score arrives, a short report under the field says what Ilya read: systems, staves, notes, rests, and measures. A caution reminds you that notes read from a picture can be wrong. Keep your own score at hand; you check the notes against it later, on Markup.

### Adding the poem

A picture of a score carries no words that Ilya can read, so the poem comes separately. Paste the Russian text into the same field.

[SHOT 4: the poem in the field, the two receipts under it]

There is no button to press. Ilya transcribes the poem as soon as you paste it, and when you type, it waits for a short pause. It also places the poem's syllables on the notes, one syllable per note.

Under the field, two receipts confirm what Ilya holds: one for the **poem**, with its lines and words, and one for the **score**, with its file name. Each has **Replace** and **Clear**.

### Naming the piece

[SHOT 5: the Piece band open, the five fields filled]

Open **Piece**. Its five fields name the song: title; opera, song cycle, or opus number; composer; poet or librettist; and translator. The composer, poet, and translator fields offer a searchable list. Type to narrow it, or choose **Not in list** to type your own.

A scanned score carries no title, so for this song you type it. A MusicXML file may carry its own header: Ilya fills the fields from it and tags each with **from score**, and **Revert to score header** restores the file's wording after you edit it. **Reset** empties the five fields and leaves the poem alone.

### Reading the text

[SHOT 6: the Text tab, with the Analysis station open and a word selected]

Choose **Text**. Each word stands in three rows: the IPA above, the Cyrillic in the middle, and a short English or French gloss below.

To study a word, open the **Analysis** station in the Input band, then click the word on the page. Analysis shows the word, its IPA, and a breakdown by syllable. Choose a letter to read the note on how Ilya arrived at its sound.

- **Stress.** Press **Syllable** under another syllable to move the stress. Ilya asks where your choice comes from: **Dictionary**, **Composer**, or **My assignment**. **Default** returns the stress to Ilya's choice.
- **ё or е.** A letter that can be either carries a small **ё ↔ е** mark. Press it to choose.
- **Gloss.** Press **Dictionary** to see the full entry and to write your own gloss, up to 20 characters. Your gloss replaces Ilya's on the page and in print.
- **Spot reconstitution.** Where a word allows it, a checkbox applies reconstitution to this word only.

After you change a word, a small circular arrow beside it undoes every change to that word.

### Checking the notes

[SHOT 7: the Markup tab with the loupe open on a note]

Choose **Markup**. The score's vocal line is drawn with the Cyrillic and the IPA under the notes.

Compare it with your own score. Click a note to open the loupe, a close-up of that note and its neighbours. The loupe has two modes.

- **Syllables.** The poem's unplaced syllables wait in a tray. Tap one to place it on the selected note.
- **Corrections.** Fix what the reader got wrong: **Duration**, **Pitch**, and **Accidental · Entry** (sharp, flat, natural, rest, tie, delete, and a new note in a gap). **Lyric** moves syllables along the line, or marks a note as a melisma that sustains the syllable before it. **Restore** returns the selected note to what Ilya read.

**Undo** and **Redo** appear in the loupe when there is something to take back. Close the loupe with its chevron or with Escape.

### Measuring your voice

[SHOT 8: the calibration, a vowel being captured]

Ilya compares a piece with your voice only after it has measured your voice. Open **Voice** and press **Calibrate**.

The calibration takes over the drawer. It asks your voice type first; **Not sure** is a fine answer. A short check of the microphone follows. Then, one vowel at a time, you sing the vowel as you usually do, stop, keep the shape, and begin again in vocal fry. Ilya measures only the fry. "Why Ilya listens to your fry", earlier in this Guide, explains why.

The summary lists what Ilya measured for each vowel, and you can retake any of them. **Add voice characteristics** asks for your range and a few optional questions for Insights; skip any you like. Press **← Back** to return to the drawer.

### Reading Insights

[SHOT 9: Insights page 1]

Choose **Insights**. With a score and a measured voice, the first page shows:

- **Phonation time:** how long you sing, and on which vowels.
- **Compatibility:** three measurements, range, passaggio crossings, and tessitura, each set against your own references and flagged where it falls outside them.
- **What is flagged**, ordered by how much time you spend singing it.

If you have not given your range, **Add your range** takes you there. A range finding offers **Try this key**. Later pages carry comments on particular notes, each with its sources.

Insights forecasts; it does not declare. "Insights forecasts, it does not declare", earlier in this Guide, says why.

### Notation preferences

[SHOT 10: the Notation station open]

The **Notation** station in the Input band holds seven switches. Each changes how the IPA is written on the page, never the analysis behind it, and they combine freely.

- **Stress acutes** mark the stressed vowel of each Cyrillic word.
- **[ə]** in place of **[ʌ]** for the unstressed central vowel.
- **[nʲ]** in place of **[ɲ]**.
- **Length markers [tː]** in place of doubled letters [tt]. This switch also sets the next one, which you can then change by itself.
- **[ʃʲː]** in place of **[ʃʲʃʲ]** for щ.
- **Reconstitution** for unstressed ⟨е⟩ after always-hard consonants, everywhere. While it is on, the per-word checkbox is not offered.
- **Open syllables**, which divides syllables so that they end in vowels. This one belongs to the song and is saved with it. Turning it on or off clears any syllable you moved by hand.

The page always shows your current settings, and what you see is what prints.

### Printing and keeping your work

**Print** sits under the page on Text, Markup, and Insights. It prints the tab you are on: the drawer and the tabs never print.

Ilya keeps your songs on this device. **Repertoire**, in the Piece band, lists them; open, rename, or delete a song there, or start a **New song**. **Export and import**, also in Piece, writes a song, or every song, to one `.ilya` file you can keep or send, and reads such a file back in.

### If you have only the poem

Paste the poem into the Input field and everything in "Adding the poem", "Naming the piece", "Reading the text", "Notation preferences", and "Printing and keeping your work" works the same way. Markup and Insights need a score, and say so until you add one.
