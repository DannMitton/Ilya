# Memo: formants by voice type, what Dann's library holds (r1, 2026-10-11)

Written by the desk (Opus) about 12:40, on Dann's question of 12:27 (does Mitton 2020 Table 5.3's fR2 scale to other voice types, as QUEUE row 17 assumes?). Searched `~/Documents/Voice Pedagogy Library` by file name for formant, voice type, classification, Cleveland, Erickson, Sundberg, Bozeman, Peterson, Hillenbrand. The web search is a separate Sonnet memo (`memo-sonnet-formants-by-voice-type_r1_2026-10-11.md`, when it returns).

1. **Titze et al. (2017; the library file says 2016), "A Formant Range Profile for Singers", JOV 31(3), doi:10.1016/j.jvoice.2016.08.014 (read off the PDF), read in full this session.** 77 subjects (54 female, 23 male), vowels in vocal fry, F1 and F2 measured by the students in Praat or VoceVista. Grouped by sex, not by voice type. "The /i/ and /u/ vowels best define the range of F2, ranging from 600 Hz to 2900 Hz in females and 500 Hz to 2600 Hz in males" (p. 382.e11). Individual quadrangle areas ranged 0.195 to 0.911 kHz² (females) and 0.337 to 0.625 kHz² (males): the spread between individuals is large. No figures by voice type.
2. **Johnson and Kempster (2010), JOV 25(5), doi:10.1016/j.jvoice.2010.05.009 (read off the PDF), read in part (introduction and method).** A secondary account of Cleveland (1977): "The averages of the first four formant frequencies of each sung vowel (i, e, a, o, and u) strongly correlated (0.90) with voice classification", in male singers. They add that Cleveland's LTAS result is cited as evidence for "a theoretical inverse relationship between vowel formants and vocal tract length", and caution that it "has not been replicated, and the sample size was extremely small". Cleveland (1977) itself is not in the library: NOT READ.
3. **Not found in the library by file name:** Cleveland (1977), Erickson, Peterson and Barney (1952), Hillenbrand et al. (1995), any table of singers' F1 and F2 by voice type. Bozeman's 2025 scans hold chapter pages, not read for this question.

**What this supports, and no more:** formants do move with voice type in male singers (Cleveland, secondary), which is the reasoning behind row 17's scaling; the library holds no figures to check the scaling against, and Titze shows that individuals differ widely, which is a reason to label every stand-in plainly.
