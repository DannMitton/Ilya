## A walkthrough with only the poem

*Deck line:* From a pasted poem to a printed sheet, with no score.

This walkthrough follows a session with only the text of a song: Gremin's Aria from Tchaikovsky's *Eugene Onegin*. You paste the poem, name the piece, read the transcription word by word, and print.

### Pasting the poem

[SHOT P1: the poem in the Input field, the poem receipt under it]

Paste the Russian text into the Input field, where it says "Paste, type, or drop your poem here." Ilya transcribes it at once. When you type instead, Ilya waits for a short pause before it transcribes. To transcribe straight away, press Command+Enter on a Mac or Ctrl+Enter elsewhere.

A receipt under the field confirms the **poem**, with its lines and words. **Replace** selects the whole poem so that your next paste replaces it. **Clear** empties it.

### Naming the piece

Open **Piece** and fill in the fields you need. "Naming the piece", in the score walkthrough, describes all five.

### Reading the page

[SHOT P2: the Text tab with the aria transcribed]

Each word stands in three rows: the IPA above, the Cyrillic in the middle, and a short gloss below. A few small marks tell you more:

- A mark at a word's corner shows where its stress came from. The legend at the foot of the page names each mark that appears on that page.
- **verify** under a word means Ilya inferred its stress and could not confirm it. Check it against your score.
- **R** marks a word with spot reconstitution.
- An arrow in place of IPA marks a word that leans on its neighbour for its stress.

On a narrow screen, **Read** turns the page into a reading aid, and **The page** brings it back.

### Studying a word

Open the **Analysis** station in the Input band, then click a word. "Reading the text", in the score walkthrough, describes what you can change: the stress, ё or е, your own gloss, and spot reconstitution.

### Setting the notation and printing

"Notation preferences" describes the seven switches. **Print** sits under the page. "Printing and keeping your work" describes printing, your Repertoire, and export.

### When you have a score

Markup and Insights need a score, and they say so until you add one. You can add one at any time: drop it on the same Input field, and Ilya places your poem's syllables on its notes.
