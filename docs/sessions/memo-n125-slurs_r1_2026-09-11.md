## The headline is §2.4, not §2.1

**A tie's drawn centre is 1.10 px, thinner than the slur's 1.3 px stroke was.**
`TIE_CENTRE_SP = 0.4` (`staff-renderer.ts:655`) puts the two control points
`sp(0.4) = 2.20` px apart at the shipping `lineGap` of 5.5
(`apps/web/src/lib/shane/engraving.ts:31`). That is not the drawn thickness. A
quadratic reaches only half way to its control point, so the gap between the two
curves is `2t(1-t) × 2.20`, greatest at `t = 0.5`, which is **1.10 px**. Sampled at
2000 steps on all ten T05 ties: 1.10 px every time, at `t = 0.5` every time. Your eye
and the source never disagreed. The taper is real and drawn at half the size its
constant names.

**Length is not the problem.** The profile `2t(1-t) × sp(TIE_CENTRE_SP)` carries no
span term, so the shortest tie on T05 (`m89-0-1`, 18.50 px) and the longest
(`m59-0-1`, 68.36 px) both peak at 1.10 px. Length changes only the distance over
which 1.10 px falls to nothing. At the page's scale of 0.95 and a device pixel ratio
of 2, the lens clears half a device pixel over 87% of its length at every span, so
about two device pixels carry the whole taper.

# Follow-on: tie and slur thickness comes from the font

Code, 2026-09-16, on your ruling that this weight is the notation font's business
and not a constant of ours. Three source files changed, plus one test fixture you

## What the font now decides

`smufl-metadata.ts` gains `tieMidpointThickness`, `tieEndpointThickness`,
`slurMidpointThickness` and `slurEndpointThickness` in `SmuflEngravingDefaults`,
in `ENGRAVING_DEFAULT_KEYS`, and in the Bravura fallback at **0.22, 0.1, 0.22 and
0.1** (`apps/web/static/fonts/bravura/bravura_metadata.json`). They fall back and
warn per key exactly as the other nine do.

The three fonts now disagree, which is the point of asking them: Bravura 0.22 and
0.1, Leland 0.21 and 0.05, Finale Maestro 0.25 and 0.05.

## The half-way correction, stated once

SMuFL's numbers are DRAWN ink. A quadratic reaches half way to its control point,
so feeding the font's number straight into a control point draws half the weight
the font asked for. `arcOutline` in `staff-renderer.ts` puts the inner control at
`2 × midThick - endThick` instead, which cancels it. Sampled across the span the
ink is

    endThick + 4u(1-u)(midThick - endThick)

so it is exactly `endThick` at each terminal and exactly `midThick` at the centre.
Ties and slurs are now one call to one function, which is Gould 151 in the code
rather than in a comment.

`TIE_CENTRE_SP` is retired. One line of its provenance survives on
`NO_FONT_ARC_MIDPOINT_SP = 0.2`, and the value is 0.2 rather than 0.4 because 0.4
was a control gap and has always drawn 0.2 sp of ink.

**With no font loaded the emitted path is byte-identical to before**, including
the absence of the joining segment, because an endpoint thickness of 0 makes that
segment zero length. The untouched primitive-mode assertions passing is the proof.

## The tests measure ink, not control points

Both shape tests in `staff-renderer.test.ts` now walk the two edges of the emitted
outline at the same x and assert the drawn thickness at the terminals and at the
centre, against a font and again with no font. No `it` block was added or removed.
A mutation check confirms they bite: feeding the font value straight into the
control point draws 1.92 px where 2.64 px is expected, and both tests fail.
