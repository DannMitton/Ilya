## New paragraphs, after the table

The dentals ⟨т⟩, ⟨д⟩, ⟨с⟩, and ⟨з⟩ are the most eager participants in the whole system. In Grayson's model, a dental takes palatalization from any palatalized consonant that follows it: a dental, a labial, a velar, or the trill.

Derwing and Priestly, writing about spoken Russian, are more cautious. Their reading rules palatalize a dental before a palatalized labial, as in зверь and четверть, but not before a palatalized velar or a palatalized trill, as in ветки and три (1980, 79–80). They also report that the Old Muscovite style treats a dental before a palatalized labial or velar as optional (1980, 84). Grayson took Derwing and Priestly's description of the Old Muscovite style and resolved its options toward softening, as a singer's rule. His worked example сестрёнка ("little sister") palatalizes the whole cluster ⟨стр⟩ (Grayson 2012, 210), and his practical advice is to palatalize the entire cluster, except for the always-hard consonants (2012, 310). In correspondence, he described Russian Stage pronunciation, from which lyric diction borrows, in the same terms (Grayson, personal communication, 25 February 2026).
