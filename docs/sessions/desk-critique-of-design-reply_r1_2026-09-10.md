## What Design did that the desk did not

1. **The phone landing (walkthrough 1.3 and 3.4, both marked FAILED).** On a
   phone the drawer fills the width, so pressing Transcribe and fit, or
   Finish, changes nothing in view except the button going from filled to
   outline, "which also reads as the button broke". Design's proposal: on a
   phone the primary action of a step lands the singer on its result, and
   Back returns them. The desk's thesis never saw this because the desk was
   looking at a desk. It is the single most valuable finding in the reply,
   and it is a ruling for Dann: it touches the portrait C ruling of
   2026-08-18 (the reading aid) and PRODUCT.md, and must be read against
   them before it is built.
2. **The stale state.** "Done is not permanent": edit the poem after
   transcribing, or flip a Notation toggle, and the page no longer matches
   the words, and nothing shows it. Partly answered by the tree Design could
   not see: N.112 transcribes as you type, so the poem case is gone; the
   toggle case is exactly N.119, already numbered. Design's frame, "2 of 7
   changed" on the Text line, is the right state form for it.
3. **The line is the door.** Frame B: the syllable line's row, opened, also
   opens Corrections at the boxed syllable, so the syllable and its hands
   are on screen together. And Design's NOT ESTABLISHED item, "whether
   tapping a syllable moves the current position", is established: it does
   (`SyllableStation.svelte:129`, `+page.svelte:4150`), so the door is half
   built already.
4. **Piece collapsed on an empty drawer.** The thesis "the drawer is a path"
   is false of Piece, a receipt that fills itself. Frame A opens only Input
   on an empty drawer; Piece and Score markup show band and nothing. Right,
   and the desk's rule 1 is corrected with it: the state line is the
   COLLAPSED form; open, the content is the state.
5. **Rule 3 corrected.** Unplaced syllables are the singer's content in
   tertiary ink; "committed is black" is true only once placed. Design
   names the WCAG 1.4.1 risk (a grey syllable carries state by hue alone;
   the count beside it is the word) and asks Dann whether a per-syllable
   word is owed. Honest, and a ruling.
6. **"Calibrate."** Every screenshot shows Re-calibrate; a first-time singer
   is offered a redo. Real gap; one string, French owed.
7. **The removals list (4c.5).** Each one a subtraction; most are right.

## Where the desk disagrees, with reasons

1. **Reordering inside Corrections by state (frame B2).** Design swaps the
   lyric group above the note tools "while the join is unfinished" and back
   after. A control that moves is a control a singer cannot find twice, and
   the slate's own grouping rule is "station order invariant". The finding
   underneath is right (three rows of note tools sit between the syllable
   and its arrow); the fix is the lyric hands FIRST in Corrections, always,
   because placing words is the singer's most frequent job there and fixing
   an engraving the rarest. Fixed order, lyric first, no mode.
2. **Striking the METADATA label on the Piece band.** Design read it as the
   band naming itself twice. It is a door: the button that opens the
   metadata body (`Drawer.svelte:444-456`, `aria-expanded`, drawn "at every
   size" by ruling). Keep the door; whether it should be a chevron rather
   than a word is a taste question for Dann, not a removal.
3. **Dropping the ⓘ on Russian-o.** It carries the ruled vowel naming
   (`Russian-o` / `o russe`, CONTRACT §6: do not rename a vowel) and its
   explanation. Design is right that one row wearing a control breaks
   "siblings behave identically"; the answer is to move the explanation
   into the table's caption, not to lose it.
4. **Removing Choose a file once a score is in.** Replace covers replacing
   the score, but a singer with a score and no poem may still want to
   choose a poem PDF or photograph. Keep it until the empty-poem case is
   walked; Design did not have the "score first" ruling (N.121: the file's
   words fill the box, "from score"), which changes this case.
5. **The two empty-state sentences.** "Type or paste the poem." and "Drop
   the score here." are good and simpler than the shipping hint, but they
   presume the poem comes first, which N.121 no longer assumes: a score may
   come first and fill the box. Adopt the shape; the wording is Dann's,
   French owed.

## What Design could not know, now supplied

- Tapping a syllable moves the SABB (above).
- The hands and the line read one map (`doc.pairings`), so the count and
  the line cannot disagree.
- The ← → row above the note tools moves the taken note (the loupe's
  locator, N.113b).
- N.112 makes the poem live; N.119 is the toggle case; N.121 rules the
  score-first case; N.118 puts punctuation on placed slots.
- The two 44 px exemptions are the table-of-contents rows and the stress
  circles (E.36); the text verbs are not covered and are a standing gap.

## Impression

Design did what it was asked: it sat in the chair and found the two places
the desk could not see from a desk, the phone landing and the stale state,
and it corrected the thesis where it was naive without discarding it. Its
frames are the desk's grammar applied, which is the outcome the brief
wanted. Where it overreached it did so by not having the tree, and every
such case is listed above with the fact that settles it. Adopt the
walkthrough method as standing practice for every surface.
