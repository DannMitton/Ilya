## 1. What shipped

**I extracted the scaler rather than copying it.**
`apps/web/src/lib/components/Paper/PageFit.svelte` is new and holds the whole
fit: the measured container, the scale, the reserved height, the out-of-flow
placement, and the print guard that undoes all four. `Paper.svelte` and
`VoiceProfilePane.svelte` both render their page stack inside it. Both Studio
documents miniaturize identically because they miniaturize through one file.

`VoiceProfilePane` takes an `isMobile` prop, mirroring `Paper`'s, threaded from
`+page.svelte`. Its pages keep their true 816 by 1056 geometry, their header
block, their shadow, and their colophon, exactly as the transcription's do.

**One gutter, declared once.** `--portrait-gutter: 24px` in `app.css`. It is
spent in two declarations and shows on three sides:

- `.main-content`'s horizontal padding on the phone (`+page.svelte`) gives the
  left and the right.
- `.desk-head`'s bottom padding on the phone (`DeskHead.svelte`) gives the top.

The page then fills the desk that is left, so **the width and the scale follow
the token** and nothing hard-codes a percentage. `PORTRAIT_PAGE_SHARE = 0.74`
is deleted. A change to the one number moves all three sides and resizes both
pages with them.

**One guard I added that the brief did not ask for.** `PageFit` clamps the
scale at 1, because this fits a page to a desk narrower than the page and is
not a zoom. `fit` is a runtime width test that a caller can hold stale across
a resize, and I measured a stale `true` at a 1280px viewport enlarging the
sheet to 1216 by 1573. The clamp cannot change the phone, where the desk is
always narrower than 816.

I did not run `git`.

## 3. Where the tree beat this brief

### The marked score's page was reflowed by two rulings, not one

The brief says the pane "was not given" a scaler. That is true and it is not
the whole cause. Two live media queries were actively reflowing its pages, and
a scaler alone would have fought them.

**N.44 (12 August) reflowed the envelope page**: `width: 100% !important`,
`height: auto !important`, and the content window taken out of its absolute
frame. Its own comment names the cause exactly: `PageFooter` went
`position: static` under the same breakpoint, so with an absolutely positioned
body the footer was the only child left in the flow and rendered at the top of
the sheet.

**N.73 portrait C deleted that footer rule.** So N.44's cause has not existed
since the previous commit, and the cure it justified is what was making this
page overflow: the title running off the sheet and the empty state sitting over
it, which is what you walked. I deleted the N.44 block and said so at the site.

### N.46 is half retired and half reinforced, and you should rule on the half I kept

**Shape A had two halves.** One deferred the NOTATION to landscape. The other
reflowed the score and commentary pages, "the same treatment the envelope took
for N.44".

I deleted the second half, for N.44's reason plus your C2 ruling that this
document's page is a miniature like the transcription's. `width: 100%`,
`height: auto`, the commentary window's `position: static`, and N.45's closed
seam are gone from that block.

**I kept the first half, and under C2 it matters more rather than less.** The
withholding exists because the stave may not be scaled down, which is your
standing ruling. A fitted page scales the stave with everything else on it, so
if the withholding went, C2 would be scaling a stave by 0.4.

**NAMED CONSEQUENCE, and it is yours to rule on.** A score page in portrait is
now a whole letter-proportioned page carrying its header, its footer, its
legend, its attribution, its rotate notice, and a deliberate hole where the
notation is not drawn. It is mostly empty by construction. That is what the two
rulings compose to, and it is not something either of them decided. I did not
invent a third behaviour to smooth it over. **I could not walk it**: it needs an
ingested score, and the reason is in §4.

### The desk's horizontal padding had to become the gutter

Your arithmetic is the viewport width minus twice the gutter, which is 327 at
375. The desk's own 0.5 rem padding sat inside that, so leaving it in place and
insetting the page by 24 more would have landed the page on 311. Spending the
gutter as the desk's padding gives your number exactly and puts the value in
one place instead of two. It also moves the desk head, which is why the head is
now flush with the sheet.
