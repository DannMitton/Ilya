# Draft: N.182, how Ilya suggests a key (r1, 2026-10-11)

A dated draft, not a ruling. Designed with Dann in conversation from 13:38 (tether 22, 2026-10-09: the singer's need first, one question at a time, before anything is drawn). Number: DESK DEFAULT. Wording: the desk's; substance: Dann's unless marked.

## Why (Dann, 13:38 to 13:43)

Not a key with every challenge removed, but a well-positioned suggestion that lets the singer focus on, examine, and consent to the challenges they are willing to accept. A range challenge may suit one singer's goals, a passaggio challenge another's; an accomplished singer may not find challenging what Ilya would call a challenge. Designed from the singer's experience, not from everything Ilya can compute: sensitive to overwhelm, superfluous information, and prose that means nothing in practice. Not a black box: Ilya explains, and the Guide explains how (INBOX, 13:40).

## Principles

1. **The number of keys follows the data.** Workable keys are grouped by the set of challenges each brings; one key is shown per distinct set, the smallest move within it (Dann, 13:43; the grouping is the desk's codification).
2. **Sometimes no key.** If the written key already suits the voice, or no other key changes the challenges in a way that matters, Ilya says so in one line and offers nothing. **And if no acceptable transposition offers a workable compromise, Ilya says so, and the singer decides whether to pursue the piece or move on to other repertoire** (Dann, 13:45). Relates to the existing "unlikely" verdict (`apps/web/src/lib/insights/singer-page.ts:70`, `isUnlikely`).
3. **Each key is qualified by its differences only.** One line, musician's terms, only what the singer would act on; no line that is true of every key; no figure that does not help a decision (Dann asked whether qualifying helps or is noise, 13:43; the desk's answer).
4. **At most three keys.** The desk's proposal against overwhelm; Dann's to strike.
6. **Data first, label second** (Dann, 13:53): *"suggestion reads the singer's own data first, and their label secondarily as corroboration or contrast."* The typed range, tessitura, and passaggi decide; the declared voice type only corroborates, or, where it disagrees, is noted as a contrast (the courtesy check, QUEUE row 9, is its natural home).
7. **The singer may look further.** On request, keys beyond the two-semitone margin are shown, each with its range challenge named plainly (agreed 13:54).
5. **The singer consents.** Ilya presents, the singer chooses; "Try this key" applies it on Markup.

## Open questions, in order

- Q2, AGREED 13:47: **a key's challenges are pitch-dependent only**, because transposition does not change the text: a note beyond the range; time at or above the primo or secondo passaggio; held notes above the secondo; vowels sung past their turning point ([ɨ] included once its turning point is its own, not derived). Linguistic difficulty (clusters, palatalization, [ɨ] density) is a piece-level portrait, a separate idea (INBOX, 13:47). Dann proposed the linguistic elements; the desk critiqued; Dann agreed. - Q3 AMENDED 13:49 to 13:50 by Dann: **vowels past their turn are not a challenge.** Dann: *"vowels past their turn (whoop timbre) is not necessarily a problem for the treble singers who have normalized that timbre"*; *"Let's not pathologize what's normal for a large cohort of singers"*; *"building a low-voice norm is great for low voices, not so great for everybody else"*; *"We can comment on sung pitches exceeding formant values, but let's be careful not to present this as an extraordinary event to voices where it is commonplace."* So a key's **challenges are two: range and passaggio time**; how much sits past the vowels' turn is **described neutrally**, as information, only where keys differ meaningfully, never framed as unusual for a voice in which it is commonplace. Earlier, Q3 AGREED 13:48 (option 1 of three, the desk's recommendation): **three challenges** a key line can name: range; passaggio time (a long hold above the secondo is the example the line names, not a separate challenge); vowels past their turn.
- Q4, AGREED 13:51 (option 1 of three, the desk's recommendation): **range is a named challenge within two semitones beyond the singer's range** ("one note a tone above your range"); a key reaching farther is not workable.
