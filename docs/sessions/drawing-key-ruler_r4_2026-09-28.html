The whole page stays in view and redraws as the selection moves, so the singer judges the song, not a bar. Each stop is a key a singer can be handed: a signature of seven accidentals or fewer. Where two such keys sound the same (B and C♭, C♯ and D♭, F♯ and G♭; in minor, G♯ and A♭, D♯ and E♭, A♯ and B♭), each has its own stop, close together under a faint bracket; where only one is real, there is one stop (F, never E♯). The readout names the interval as heard, so C♯ and D♭ both read "up a semitone", and the key name carries the spelling (ruling 14). The major ruler is shown; a minor song gets the minor keys. G♯ major is not a practical key, so a major song reaches A♭ at the tritone ends. The printed key sits in its grey box, always one tap away. The circle is the current choice, opening at Ilya's recommendation; the dotted key is the runner-up. Nothing evaluative appears when the singer moves off the recommendation. The strip never prints. On a phone it docks at the bottom edge, like the loupe's dock.
