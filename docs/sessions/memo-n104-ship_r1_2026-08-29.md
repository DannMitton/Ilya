#### One thing the narrower bar does bring closer, and it did not bite

`staff-renderer.ts:1258` composes the H-bar from the face's three glyphs only
when the drawn bar is at least as wide as the two terminals together,
`restHBarLeft` 2.692 plus `restHBarRight` **2.692**, which is **5.384

U+E4EE, centred.

> **DESK CORRECTION, 2026-08-29, before the commit.** This memo first read
> 2.832 and 5.524. `restHBarRight`'s bounding box is SW `[0.14, -1.028]`, NE
> `[2.832, 1.028]`, so 2.832 is its NE x and its WIDTH is 2.692. Read out of
> `apps/web/static/fonts/finale-maestro/FinaleMaestro.json` by the desk this
> session. **The code is right and only the memo was wrong**: `:1257`
> computes `wR = sp(R.bBoxNE[0] - R.bBoxSW[0])`. The error is conservative,
> so every conclusion below stands and the margins are slightly larger than
> reported.

`measureSp` 8 less two insets of 1.25 is 5.5 stave-spaces, which is under that
threshold, so I went looking for a render that crosses it. A system justifies
to the full line unless it is the last and fills less than 0.75 of it, so the
case to fear is a run ending the piece on a short system. I built three: the
vocal notes stripped from the last two, last three, and last five source
measures.

| the tail I stripped | the run | its system | that system's fill | the mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| measures 17 to 18 | `16-17` count 2 | `15-17`, 304.03 px | 48.7 percent, unjustified | 48.24 px, composed |
| measures 16 to 18 | `15-16` count 2 | `12-16`, 671.91 px | justified | 48.25 px, composed |
| measures 14 to 18 | `13-17` count 5 | `12-17`, 303.52 px | 48.6 percent, unjustified | 48.25 px, composed |

**Every one composed.** Each mark carries U+E4EF, U+E4F0 and U+E4F1, never
U+E4EE. The narrowest drawn bar any of them produced is 48.25 px, which is 8.77
stave-spaces, so the margin to the fallback is **3.39** stave-spaces
(3.25 before the correction above).

**DESK ADDITION, the rule this measurement was reaching for.** The drawn mark
runs about `measureSp` + 0.77 stave-spaces, derived from this memo's two data
points (8 gives 8.77, 12 gives about 12.8) rather than measured. The composed
H-bar therefore survives down to a `measureSp` of about **4.6**. A future
ruling of 5 clears by roughly 0.4; a ruling of 4 falls back. **The fallback is
graceful, not broken**: `:1285-1288` draws the complete fixed-width `restHBar`
centred, overhanging the insets rather than distorting the face's
proportion.

The reason 8.77 is not 5.5: `layoutColumns` spends `measureSp × lineGap` plus
`BARLINE_ROOM` across the run (`staff-renderer.ts:654`, `:677`), so a tacet
measure's natural width is about 11.3 stave-spaces rather than 8, and 8 is its
share of the line rather than its floor. **The margin is real but it is smaller
than it was**: the same three tails at 12 give a drawn bar of about 12.8
stave-spaces. Nothing here is broken and nothing needs a change; it is the one
number a future `measureSp` ruling should be checked against, and it is why a
ruling of 4 or 5 would need this measurement repeated rather than assumed.

### What each plate costs in vertical room

The renderer crops a system at `top = floor(min(highestInk, staffTop) - sp(1))`
(`staff-renderer.ts:1906`). **These costs are a projection, not a measurement**:
they are what the crop would become if a real bar number fed `highestInk` the
way the tacet numeral does at `:1322`. Nothing is built, so nothing was measured
doing it. The ink tops are measured, on the rendered plates, through the
canvas's own `actualBoundingBoxAscent` against the embedded face.

**On the clean system**, whose crop top is 68 and which carries nothing else in
the band:

| plate | the number's ink top | projected crop top | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| italic 1.6 stave-spaces | 73.69 | 68 | **0 px** |
| italic 2.0 stave-spaces | 72.24 | 66 | **2 px** |
| italic 2.4 stave-spaces | 70.79 | 65 | **3 px** |
| italic semibold 2.0 | 72.31 | 66 | **2 px** |
| roman 2.0 | 72.24 | 66 | **2 px** |

**On the crowded system, every plate costs nothing at all.** Its crop top is 63,
set by the tacet numeral, whose ink top is 69.23. Every bar number in sections B,
C and D has an ink top below that:

| plate | the number's ink top | projected crop top | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| post-rest, clearance 0.6 stave-spaces | 74.55 | 63 | 0 px |
| post-rest, clearance 1.0 | 72.35 | 63 | 0 px |
| post-rest, clearance 1.4 | 70.15 | 63 | 0 px |
| the parenthesized `(17)` | 71.39 | 63 | 0 px |
| the system-start `13` in D | 72.24 | 63 | 0 px |

**That is a finding rather than an accident.** A post-rest number exists only on
a system that already carries a multibar rest, and the tacet numeral already
reaches higher than any of these. So the post-rest number is free in vertical
room by construction, and only the system-start number on a system with no tacet

### What section D shows, which prose could not

**The post-rest bar number and the tuplet count are the same face, the same
size, the same slope, and the same height.** The tuplet count is Source Serif 4
italic at 11 px, inherited from the page's root `font-family` at
`staff-renderer.ts:1908` and set italic at `:1126`. A bar number drawn italic at
the middle size is the identical mark. Their ink tops differ by **0.89 px**
(72.35 against 73.24). On this system the only thing that tells a reader which
is which is the tuplet's bracket.

Gould separates the two by position rather than by face, and on this system
position does separate them: 98.99 px, or 18 stave-spaces, from the bar number's
right ink to the bracket's left end.

**Three things that follow, and all three are predictions rather than
measurements**, because no document in this project contains them:

1. A tuplet beginning on the first note of the measure after a multibar rest
   would put the two italic numerals within a notehead of each other, with only
   the bracket to tell them apart.
2. The tuplet's bracket runs at y = 77 on this system, which is inside the bar
   number's own ink band of 72.35 to 79.50. A post-rest number over a bracket
   would cross it.
3. When a multibar rest ends a system, the measure after it is the next
   system's first measure, which already takes a number under the system-start
   rule. The two rules then produce one number rather than two.
