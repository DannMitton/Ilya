## What the singer sees

1. On a song read from a scan or a photograph, Markup draws the slurs, accents, and fermatas the print carries, where the print carries them.
2. On a song from a MusicXML file, Markup draws every slur and note marking the file carries.
3. A melisma's slur is the printed slur. Ilya does not add one of its own to a score that prints slurs.
4. A fermata over a rest is drawn, a whole-bar rest included.
5. The markings print with the page and appear in the loupe: they are notation, not controls (`CONTRACT.md` §8).
6. The words under the notes never move lower to make room for a marking.

## What to build

**A. The parser keeps slurs, and fermatas on rests.**
1. Each event carries its printed slur ends from `<notations><slur>`: type (`start` or `stop`), and `placement` when the file gives one; a note that stops one slur and starts another keeps both, in document order. **The parser pairs the ends over the whole voice line** and stores the partner on each end, after the tie's own partner pass (`TieInfo.partnerEventId`, `types.ts:615-619`; `musicxml-parser.ts:640-655`). Name the field to fit `types.ts`.
2. **Pairing policy, for reader output that is not tidy:** a start of a number already open abandons the open one (one parser warning) and opens the new; a stop with nothing open is dropped (one warning); an end on a rest is dropped (one warning); a slur whose two ends land on one note draws nothing.
2a. Grace notes are dropped by the parser today (`musicxml-parser.ts:707-709`; N.187 draws them later). A slur start on a dropped grace note moves to the next kept note; a slur stop on one moves to the last kept note before it. The usual grace-to-main slur then has both ends on one note and draws nothing (A.2), which is right until N.187 draws grace notes.
3. A fermata on a rest is kept, as on a note, and `<fermata type="inverted">` fills `FermataInfo.placement` (`types.ts:630`).
4. **The join keeps a rest that carries a fermata:** `silent()` (`apps/web/src/lib/omr/join-pages.ts:295-298`) no longer strips a rest with `<fermata>`; that bar then holds one rest with its fermata and breaks the tacet run (DESK DEFAULT: a fermata bar is not part of a multirest).
5. **One flag, computed once:** whether the voice line carries any printed slur, set in the parser's output (or in `paginateScore`) and passed to the renderer as a `StaffRenderOptions` field, so the page, the print, and the loupe agree (B.5).
6. Nothing else in the parser's output changes. Move the notations block into a new module if that keeps `musicxml-parser.ts` at or under 1168.
7. MNX (the `.musx` path): report whether MNX carries slurs (`packages/score-parser/src/mnx-parser.ts:23` says slurs are not parsed there). Do not build it here.

**B. Markup draws the printed slurs.**
1. Draw each slur between its paired ends. An end whose partner is in another system draws a half slur to that system's content edge. This is new for the page: a tie across a break is not drawn there today. In the loupe, tag the leaving half `data-slur-runon` and treat it as `Loupe.svelte:1089-1091` treats `data-tie-runon`, or the clip cuts it. A partner missing from the whole line (a note deleted in Corrections) is a missing partner: draw nothing, raise no error.
2. Direction: the file's `placement` when given; otherwise the rule the syllabic slur already follows (Gould rule 71, snippet). A printed slur and a tie over the same notes curve in opposite directions (Gould rule 174, snippet): **printed slur spans fill the same `slurredIdx` set before the tie pass** (`staff-renderer.ts:3189`, `:3207`), so ties under printed slurs keep the opposite bow.
3. Ends: at the noteheads, each end at its own head, at the tie's head distance (`staff-renderer.ts:913-914`). `arcOutline` draws both ends level, so add a sibling function that takes `y1` and `y2` for printed slurs; ties and the derived slur keep `arcOutline`, so their tests stay byte-stable (DESK DEFAULT). Dann's end-point rule decides the exception: where both ends at heads would force a steep or deep curve to clear an inner note, move an end off its head toward the stem until the arc is shallow. Write the threshold as a named constant with a comment saying where it came from.
4. The curve's middle stays a parabola, as `arcOutline`'s is. The cubic "shoulder" in the same `OPEN.md` section is a later fine-tuning step (DESK DEFAULT).
5. **The derived melisma slur, ruled with Dann 2026-10-19 20:26 to 20:28:** on a read scan (`parsed.source.origin === 'homr-musicxml-from-image'`) it is never drawn; Markup shows only the slurs the print carries (Dann 20:26: *"it is better to replicate a printed error than to silently fix it"*; `PRODUCT.md:808-810`). On an uploaded MusicXML or MNX file it is drawn only when A.5's flag says the file carries no printed slur (DESK DEFAULT, his to overrule). The extender, the hyphens, and the melisma's left-aligned syllable are unchanged everywhere.

**C. Markup draws the note markings and fermatas.**
1. One glyph per marking, from the `Above` and `Below` pairs listed in the facts; `detached-legato` is `articTenutoStaccato`; `breath-mark` is `breathMarkComma`; `caesura` is `caesura`. Add each to `SMUFL_CODEPOINTS` at the codepoints listed.
2. Placement (DESK DEFAULT, from Gould rule 74, snippet): at the notehead on the side away from the stem **only when the glyph's bottom is at or above `staffBottom`**; otherwise above the stave. The words under the notes never move. Where a marking and a slur meet at a note, the marking sits between the notehead and the slur (Gould rule 70, snippet): **draw markings before slurs**, so the slur's clearance walk (`staff-renderer.ts:3269-3283`) lifts over them; reuse that walk for stem tips, beams, and the turning layer. A staccato, staccatissimo, or tenuto at the notehead sits in a space, not on a line (DESK DEFAULT; engravers' convention, not read in Gould this session).
3. A fermata goes above the stave, centred on its note or rest (below only when `placement` says so); on a whole-bar rest, over the rest at the bar's centre. A breath mark sits above the stave, after its note.
4. **Ink:** every marking and every printed slur updates `highestInk` and `lowestInk`, as ties (`:3246-3247`) and the syllabic slur (`:3287`) do, or the viewBox clips it. A printed slur goes below the notes only when its lowest point stays within ink already there; otherwise above.
5. Markings print and appear in the loupe because the same renderer draws them; check both.

**D. Which kinds are drawn on a read scan.** One list, in one place, of the kinds drawn when **`parsed.source.origin === 'homr-musicxml-from-image'`**: **slurs, accents, fermatas**. Every other kind stays in the data and is not drawn on a read scan until a measure on Dann's proofed pages earns it. The test (DESK DEFAULT, from the principle's condition): of the marks of that kind the reader writes on the proofed pages, at least 9 in 10 are printed. Tonight's figures: slur ends 91 of 96 written (5 extra), accents 10 of 10, fermatas 4 of 4. On a MusicXML or MNX file every kind is drawn. (Do not key this on `provenance.via`: see the facts.)
