## 1. The difference set

**50 of 62 rows** have a French form that is not `same` (20 of 25 composers, 30 of 37 poets). 10 rows are `same` (Cui, Glinka, Rubinstein, Stravinsky among composers; Akhmatova, Gippius, Goethe, Heine, Pasternak, Shakespeare among poets). 2 rows are `NOT ESTABLISHED` (Bulakhov, Galina). "Same" was judged strictly: even a bare soft-sign mark BnF includes (Balmont, Delvig) counted as a difference, since it's a literal character difference from the English spelling on the list.

## 2. What I could not establish

- **Bulakhov, Pyotr** (composer, 1822–1885): no BnF authority record under Bulakhov, Boulakhov, or Bulahov matches these dates. The only BnF hit for the surname is an unrelated living violinist/conductor born 1960. Marked NOT ESTABLISHED.
- **Galina, Glafira** (poet, 1870–1942): no BnF record. French Wikipedia has an article, but it's titled "Galina Galina" — her pen name doubled — rather than a Surname,Given pair built from her real name (Glafira Nikolaevna Mamoshina). That doesn't give a clean French analogue to the list's "Galina, Glafira" entry, so this is NOT ESTABLISHED rather than a forced mapping.
- **Rachmaninoff, Sergei** (composer, 1873–1943): marked SPLIT. BnF's own authorized heading is clearly "Rachmaninov, Serge" (single f) with no "Rachmaninoff" variant appearing in the record itself, but the double-f spelling is widespread in French concert and press usage outside BnF's catalogue. I'm reporting BnF's preference as the primary answer and flagging the split rather than picking one silently.

## 3. Anything that looked wrong

Nothing in the English list's spellings or dates conflicted with what BnF's records show — every date matched exactly, and where a BnF-listed patronymic implied a fuller name (e.g., Baratynsky's patronymic confirming "Yevgeny" not a variant), it was consistent with the list.

The one thing worth flagging is about the *source*, not the list: BnF's authority file is not stylistically uniform. A sizeable minority of these headings (Arensky, Balakirev, Dargomyzhsky, Gurilev, Medtner, Prokofiev, Sviridov, Varlamov, Khomyakov, Koltsov, Marshak, Maykov, Merezhkovsky, Mey, Pleshcheyev, Polonsky among others) use BnF's ISO 9 scientific transliteration — with letters like č, š, ž, â, û and the modifier ʹ that don't occur in ordinary French typography — rather than the older "gallicized" French transliteration that Larousse, Universalis, and French Wikipedia commonly use for the same people (e.g., those sources write "Khomiakov," "Koltsov," "Prokofiev, Serge" where BnF's authorized heading is "Homâkov," "Kolʹcov," "Prokofʹev, Sergej"). Famous, long-catalogued figures (Pushkin, Tchaikovsky, Mussorgsky, Borodin, Tyutchev, Shevchenko, Glazunov, Cui) keep the classic French forms; lesser-known ones tend to carry the newer ISO heading. Since the brief instructs BnF-first without inventing a rule, I've reported the literal BnF heading in every case — but whoever builds the dropdown from this table should know that a chunk of these forms will look unfamiliar even to francophone singers, because they're a library-cataloguing convention, not the spelling used in French score editions or concert programs.
