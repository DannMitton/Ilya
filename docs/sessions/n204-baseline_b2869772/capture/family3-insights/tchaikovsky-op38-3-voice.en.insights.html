m. 11 · D4 · [u]

This [u] on D4 sits just under your secondo passaggio and a little under the first resonance (fR1) of your [u]. Here the [u] tends to open a little on its own (Miller, The Structure of Singing, 1986, p. 158); but keeping it closed tends toward whoop timbre (Bozeman, Practical Vocal Acoustics, 2025, p. 94). If it suits your voice, let it lean toward [ʊ], and notice whether the [u] keeps its colour.

You can also come at it differently. Consider easing into the note with a slight decrescendo, to see whether the [u] keeps its shape (Reid, Voice: Psyche and Soma, 1975, p. 66). You can experiment with singing “you” on a quick five-note descending scale, then dropping the [j] and singing only the [u], keeping the same shape of lips and face (Miller, Solutions for Singers, 2004, pp. 76 to 77). One thing to explore is singing the leap legato, thinking of the D4 as a note that asks for more energy, space, and depth, not simply a high one (McKinney, The Diagnosis and Correction of Vocal Faults, 1994, p. 189).

Discuss these approaches with your voice teacher.
