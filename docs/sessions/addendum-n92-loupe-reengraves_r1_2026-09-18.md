## 2. THE CORRECTION. Width is not the fix. Relative spacing is

The brief's section 1.0.2 ends by saying the extra width "is the room the carets
have been missing all night." **That sentence is the desk's and it is wrong, so
do not build against it.**

**Widening the loupe changes `scale`, not the spacing between events.** Every
quantity in the collision is proportional: the clearance is 1.2 line-gaps, the
arrowhead's base is 0.34 of one, the caret's stroke is the stave's own line
width, and the squircle is drawn from the note's ink. Scale them all up together
and the overlaps are exactly as they were. **The 27 gaps are scale-invariant. A
wider window alone changes none of them.**

**What fixes them is wider RELATIVE spacing:** more distance between one event
and the next, measured in line-gaps, than the page's own engraving gives. That is
what re-engraving buys and what a crop can never buy.

**So the two rulings do different jobs, and you need both:**

- **Re-engraving at wider relative spacing** creates the room. This is the fix.
- **The width retraction (1.0.2)** lets the result be shown at full notation size
  instead of being shrunk back down to the page's width, which would undo it.

## 3. THE INSTRUCTION. Derive the spacing, do not pick a multiplier

**Do not choose a spacing multiplier by eye.** Derive it from the requirement:

- For the held measure, compute what each gap needs: the caret's own footprint,
  plus its clearance from the ink on each side, plus, where the selected note is
  a neighbour, its squircle's stroke.
- **Re-engrave at the smallest relative spacing at which every caret takes the
  middle of its own space and no floor has to fire.** State the number you
  derived, in line-gaps, and the measure that demanded the most.
- **If a measure would then need more width than the viewport can give**, say so
  and name the measure. Do not shrink the notation to make it fit: that is what
  1.0.2 retracted. **On a device that cannot give the width, the singer turns to
  landscape or scrolls, which is N.140 and is not this brief's work.**

## 3.1 ONE MORE ACCEPTANCE ITEM. No notation glyph is ever drawn clipped

Dann, on m. 16: *"Why is there a strange half-sharp following the meter
signature?... I did not program any microtonal notation."* What is drawn is one
vertical stroke with two slanted crossbars, half of the sharp glyph, cut by the
body crop's left edge.

**This is a different class from the night's other findings. A half-drawn sharp
is Ilya making a claim about the pitch that the score does not make.** Under the
freeze rule, that is the "tells a singer something false" exception.

**Add to the acceptance scan: no notation glyph is drawn clipped, anywhere in the
loupe, on any measure. Whole or absent.** The whole-mark rule the brief states
for carets holds for every glyph the loupe draws.
