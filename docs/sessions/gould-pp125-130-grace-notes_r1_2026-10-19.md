What the pages say, paraphrased (page, heading). Items 1 to 4 decide N.187's drawing; 5 to 8 decide the slurs on grace notes (N.188 and the truth of p02 bar 8).

1. **p. 125, "Design": size.** A grace note is a small notehead with its stem shortened to about 2¼ stave-spaces; tails, beams, articulation, and accidentals are scaled down in proportion (a sharp or natural 2 spaces long instead of 3; a flat 1¾). A grace note is slightly smaller than a cue note, and a cue note is ¾ of full size. So a grace note is drawn at a little under ¾ of full size. (Dann's figure of 2026-10-18 21:39, "75-80%", recalled from Finale, sits just above hers.)
2. **p. 125: the single grace note and the group.** A single grace note is a small quaver with a diagonal stroke through its tail; the stroke is what tells it from an appoggiatura, which is the same note without the stroke. A group is joined by beams: two beams are recommended (three are common; some editions match the beam count to the number of notes); a stroke through the beam is optional, to keep a group from reading as appoggiaturas.
3. **p. 126: the stroke.** It crosses the tail or beam without touching the notehead, ideally with white space between stem, tail, and stroke; on a group it sits at the group's start. A grace note on ledger lines takes a stem long enough that the stroke clears the ledger lines.
4. **p. 126 to 127, "Stem direction".** Grace notes take up-stems wherever they sit on the stave. Exceptions: two parts on one stave (the lower part's grace notes take down-stems); double-stemmed beams; grace notes attached to a measured value (p. 129: their stems point away from the measured notes).
5. **p. 127 to 128, "Alignment".** Grace notes stand before the beat whether they are sung on it or before it; before a bar's first beat they usually stand after the barline. A group of three or more sung before the beat may stand before the barline. A trill's finishing notes stand before the barline.
6. **p. 129, "Slurs".** Grace notes are slurred to the following measured note when that is the articulation. **The slur goes below the grace note, from notehead to notehead.** With two parts on a stave, grace-note slurs curve away from the stave. A slur must not cover a ledger line; it may sit above the ledger lines instead.
7. **p. 130.** A slur goes above the notes where it would otherwise hit the measured note's accidental, and to a chord's top note (the note that resolves the ornament). Each grace-note group usually takes its own slur, even inside a longer slur ("usual (and recommended) practice"); one longer slur over both is acceptable but less clear.
8. **p. 130, "Articulation" and "Dynamics".** A grace note's articulation goes where it is most visible, usually outside the stave. A dynamic is centred under the measured note, or under the first grace note.
