A warm brown, in the same vocabulary

r1, 2026-09-13. Three values per hue, exactly as the four existing families are built: light is 40 % hue plus 60 % white (verified against all four, every hex reproduces), medium is the signature hue itself, dark is hand-set to clear 4.5 against white. Hue angles are OKLCH.

The warm side of the wheel has one long gap, from rose at 19° to sage at 130°. A brown belongs in it. Where in it is the whole question.

Every one of the twelve values is inside sRGB and every dark tier clears 4.5 on white, so the choice is purely your eye. Chestnut is the risk: at 26° from dusty rose it can read as a warmer rose rather than as a brown of its own. Tobacco is the other risk: by 75° the yellow is doing enough work that it starts to read olive. Umber sits between them and is the most evenly spaced from both neighbours.

The label ink is the fifth value, the one rose needed too: rose uses #8A5C5C at 4.69 so its small caps clear 4.5 on cream. Umber's equivalent is #7A5D40 at 5.09, with room to lighten to #806245 at 4.71 if it reads too heavy.

The name. The existing four are a plant, a flower, a flower and a pigment: sage, dusty rose, deeper lavender, quiet cobalt. Umber is a pigment, which puts it beside cobalt and keeps the register. --warm-umber follows the two-word pattern; --quiet-umber echoes cobalt's adjective if you would rather the set rhyme.
