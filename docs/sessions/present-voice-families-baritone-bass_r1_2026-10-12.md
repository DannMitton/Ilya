## The bass

**The offer:** the bass is the voice for which Dann's own markup was made. His words of 2026-10-11 22:09: a collision of fR1 and fo "in a bass voice is a notable event requiring adjustment." So for the bass the eight events stand, and event 5 (the first resonance met) is the one that matters most, because it is uncommon: fo exceeds the typical first resonance only for a few vowels at the top of the range (Wolfe et al. 2008, p. 17). Bass-baritone pitches as the default unless the singer says otherwise (DESK DEFAULT).

| # | Event | The bass's pitches | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chest carried past the primo | Bass-baritone and some lyric basses: A3 (Miller 2008, p. 79); a "low bass" A3 to D4 (Bozeman 2008, p. 60, citing Miller). The bass's modification begins at A♭3 or G3 (Miller 2008, p. 58) | as named |
| 2 | The weak note of the lower middle | No source prints one. Left without a pitch | none |
| 3 | The secondo | D4 for the bass-baritone (Miller 2008, p. 79) and for the bass (pp. 83, 85). Top of the turning zone D-D♯4 (Bozeman 2008, p. 66). **Conflict kept:** Miller's "a whole tone lower than the baritone's" (p. 79) gives D♭4 by arithmetic, not print; D. Miller 2000 gives D4 or E♭4 | as named |
| 4 | A closed vowel set high | E4 'clearly lies above the secondo' of most bass-baritones and basses; 'd'ogni' on E4 moves toward /ɔ/, /ʊ/, or /œ/ | Miller 2008, p. 80 |
| 5 | The first resonance met (fR1 against fo) | Few vowels, at the top of the range only; the draft's "little to manage over most of the range" row is why it is notable when it happens | Wolfe et al. 2008, p. 17; Herbst 2026b, p. 593 |
| 6 | Leaps that cross a register | 'conosce Bartolo': D4 reached by an octave from D3 and by a fourth from A3; an isolated passaggio note risks a sudden cover or none | Miller 2008, pp. 79, 82 |
| 7 | Load: long, loud, or high-lying writing | 'La calunnia', high-lying and full-bodied throughout, to a climactic F#4 | Miller 2008, pp. 83, 85 |
| 8 | Soft singing high in the range | As the baritone | Miller 2008, p. 126 |

**The low edge, for the bass:** notated floors in the young bass's repertoire reach D2, E2, F♯2, and C2, with C4 as a young low bass's ceiling (Ord, *JOS*, 1995, pp. 23-32). Strohbass and fry: "no real danger" in occasional use (Miller 2000, p. 52) beside "hard on the voice, not to be drilled" (Miller 1986, pp. 125-126); fry useful for low basses and not for baritones (Leyerle 1986, cited in Nix et al. 2005, p. 53).
