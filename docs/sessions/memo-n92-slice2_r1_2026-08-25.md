## What shipped

**Leg 1, the policy, one pure function.** `spellPitch` in
`packages/score-parser/src/transposition.ts:190`, with its context type at
`:111`. It takes a sounding MIDI number and a context of `{ key?, previous? }`
and returns a `Pitch`. Three tiers, in order:

1. A diatonic note of the key is spelled as the key spells it
   (`diatonicSpellings`, `:146`). This is what lets C sharp major write its own
   B sharp and C flat major its own C flat, so `atOctave` (`:163`) subtracts the
   alteration before dividing, or those two spellings would land an octave out.
2. A chromatic note spells to the key's side: `FLAT_SPELLING` (`:52`) in flat
   keys, the existing `SPELLING` table in sharp keys. A key of no sharps and no
   flats takes the sharp side, which is what the reader already did.
3. With no key at all, the melodic fallback: the spelling that makes a plain
   interval from the previous note (`isPlainInterval`, `:172`, against
   `PLAIN_INTERVAL`, `:143`), with direction as the tie-break.

`transposePitch` (`:63`) keeps the sharps-only table and is now named in its own
comment as the opt-out for callers that want height and have no engraving
opinion. `transposeScore` is untouched. Nothing in the app holds a second
speller: the pair of tables that lived in `recognized-to-musicxml.ts` is gone,
and `STEP_SEMITONE` is exported once from `overlay-engine.ts:57` rather than
copied.

**Leg 2, verbs and nudges.** `apps/web/src/lib/shane/correction.ts`:

- `flatPitch` (`:168`), `sharpPitch` (`:173`), `naturalPitch` (`:178`).
  Cumulative, capped at doubles, natural resets to the plain letter. The letter
  and the octave never move, so sound follows spelling. A verb that would change
  nothing returns the same object, so a capped click records no correction.
- `semitonePitch` (`:144`) now takes a `SpellingContext` and routes through
  `spellPitch`. `stepPitch` (`:119`) is unchanged; its comment no longer claims
  `transposePitch` is the only speller, because that sentence had gone stale.
- A hand spelling is never rewritten. The verbs write a full `Pitch` into the
  correction map and nothing downstream re-spells it. Asserted at
  `correction.test.ts`, "keeps a hand spelling the policy would have spelled the
  other way".

The page supplies the context at `apps/web/src/routes/+page.svelte:467`,
`spellingContextFor`. The key comes from the note's OWN measure
(`Measure.keySignature`), not from the score's first key signature, so a
mid-score change of key spells the notes after it correctly. The accidental
handler is at `:486`.

**Leg 3, the palette in the drawer.** Three tools in the NOTATION anchor beside
ship 3's verbs: `CorrectionControls.svelte:91` for the table, `:144` for the
row. Sage accent, glyph plus word, glyph `aria-hidden` so a screen reader says
"Flat" rather than "music flat sign". No `aria-pressed`: the verbs are
cumulative, so none of the three is ever "on". Nothing landed on the paper.

**French**, `apps/web/src/lib/i18n.ts:171-173`, exactly the approved table.
Verified rendering in the running app as `♭ Bémol`, `♮ Bécarre`, `♯ Dièse`.

## The policy's wiring point in the ingestion path

**The call is at
`apps/web/src/lib/shane/ingestion/recognized-to-musicxml.ts:309`**, inside the
note loop, replacing the local `spellPitch(midi, answers.fifths)`. The
import is at `:37` and the note explaining the change is at `:119`. The key
comes from `answers.fifths`, the value the singer confirms at intake, so there
is always a tonal anchor there and the previous note is deliberately not passed.

That is where MIDI first becomes a spelling on the page-read path. It is not
where MIDI first becomes a `Pitch`: the `Pitch` object is built one step later
by `MusicXmlScoreParser`, from the `<step>`, `<alter>`, and `<octave>` this file
writes. Spelling before the MusicXML text is what keeps the reader's output an
ordinary score at the existing ingest seam.

## The corpus spelling comparison

**The check.** Take the vocal part of Dann's own engravings of Mussorgsky's
*Sunless*, all six songs, 1934 notes, at
`tools/e16-harness/output/*/mxl_extract/score.musicxml`. For each note, compute
its sounding MIDI from the engraved spelling, ask the policy to spell it from
the key alone, and compare with what Dann engraved. The scripts live in the
session scratchpad, not in the tree, and they import the shipped policy rather
than reimplementing it.

**Before and after, same corpus, same method:**

| | agreement with the engraving |
|---|---|
| the retired tables | 1515 of 1934, 78.3% |
| the policy as shipped | **1801 of 1934, 93.1%** |

Every point of that gain is in *On the river*, seven sharps, where tier 1 now
writes the key's own B sharp, E sharp, and A sharp instead of naturals: 875 of
1174 before, 1161 of 1174 after. No song got worse.

**Per song, as shipped:** *Within four walls* 80/96, *You did not recognize me*
59/68, *Finished is the noisy idle day* 145/222, *Be bored* 116/116, *Elegy*
240/258, *On the river* 1161/1174.

**Checked by eye, not by the script.** *Within four walls*, measure 2, the
engraved line reads A A A B flat F F under "Ком нат ка тес на я". The signature
is two sharps. Dann engraved the fourth note B flat; the policy, spelling a
chromatic note to the key's sharp side, says A sharp. The parser read that
measure correctly (`<step>B</step>`, `<alter>-1</alter>`, `<octave>3</octave>`,
syllable "тес"), so the disagreement is the policy's, not the script's.

**The residual, and it is systematic.** The 133 disagreements fall into two
families:

1. **A flat-side chromatic note in a sharp key** (B flat and A flat in the two
   D major songs, and most of *Finished is the noisy idle day*). Tier 2 sends
   these to the key's side by ruling, and Mussorgsky writes them the other way.
2. **A note whose spelling the harmony decides** (E sharp, C double sharp,
   G double sharp in *Elegy*; C natural and F natural where the key of seven
   sharps would say B sharp and E sharp in *On the river*). Neither tier can
   reach these: the policy sees the key and the note, never the chord.

**A measurement, offered as evidence and not as a proposal.** Running the same
corpus with one change, that a note the key signature does not contain is
spelled by the melodic fallback from the previous sung note instead of by the
key's side, gives **1888 of 1934, 97.6%**. Per song: 94/96, 66/68, 212/222,
115/116, 240/258, 1161/1174. It costs one note in *Be bored* and gains 88
elsewhere. **This is not what shipped.** The brief ruled the key's side, so the
key's side is what is in the tree, and this line exists so the ruling can be
revisited against a number rather than an impression.

The same corpus decided one constant inside the shipped fallback. `PLAIN_INTERVAL`
(`:143`) calls a chromatic inflection of one letter NOT plain, so A up to the
flat sixth spells B flat rather than A sharp. The looser reading of that row
scores 96.2% where the strict one scores 97.6%, and *Within four walls* measure
2 is the case that separates them.

## NOT ESTABLISHED

- **Gould rule 66 was not quoted.** The rules document is not in this
  repository; the tree holds only references to it. The policy implements the
  brief's paraphrase of rule 66, not the rule's own words. Everything the tiers
  do beyond that paraphrase is JUDGEMENT, and the one number in it
  (`PLAIN_INTERVAL`'s unison row) was settled against the corpus rather than
  against Gould.
- **The melodic fallback is unreachable from the app today.** Both parsers give
  every measure a `KeySignature`, so tier 3 fires only where a caller passes no
  key. It is exercised by unit tests and by the corpus measurement above, and by
  nothing a singer can do.
- **The corpus is one composer, one engraver, one cycle.** 1934 notes of
  Mussorgsky as Dann engraves him. Whether 93.1% holds on Rachmaninoff or on a
  publisher's plate is not established.
- **Tier 1 is occasionally too eager, and by how much is not established.** In
  seven sharps the policy writes B sharp and E sharp where Dann engraved C
  natural and F natural, three times in *On the river*. Whether that is Dann's
  taste, the passage's harmony, or a fault in tier 1 needs a look at those bars,
  which this work did not do.
- **Behaviour on an existing stored correction was not tested.** Corrections
  written before this ship carry a full `Pitch` and are read back unchanged, and
  no migration is needed by inspection, but no stored map from before the ship
  was loaded and checked.
- **The deploy walk is not done**, so this is WRITTEN. Nothing here has been in
  front of Dann's own hands.

## The walk, on a deploy

Branch alias `ilya-git-shane-dannmittons-projects.vercel.app`.

1. Open a score in a flat key and pick a note in the drawer's CORRECT THE READ.
2. Press **Down a semitone**. The name should show the flat-side spelling, not
   the sharp one: D becomes D flat in E flat major.
3. Press **Flat**, then **Flat** again. The name reaches a double flat.
4. Press **Flat** a third time. Nothing changes.
5. Press **Natural**. The plain letter comes back.
6. Reload the page. The corrected spelling is still there.
7. Look at the three tools: they sit in the NOTATION anchor, and on the phone
   they are the same height as every other button in that cluster.
