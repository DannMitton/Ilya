## How the draft was made

Richter prints the Latin in Latin letters and transcribes it the way a Russian
reads Latin: consonants before ⟨i⟩ are palatalized, and unstressed vowels are
not reduced. His notation is his own (a mark under a consonant for
palatalization, ⟨c⟩ for [ts]). The desk converted each word into Grayson's
inventory: a palatalized consonant takes ʲ, except that a palatalized n is ɲ
(Grayson's default); a hard l is ɫ; stress is ˈ before the syllable. **Every symbol
is inside Grayson's inventory except [w],** in four words.

**Read from the page images; the diacritics are small.** The desk read the Latin
lines again from full-resolution crops of pp. 44, 45, and 47. The five rows that were hard to read (ensis, vectis, mensis, sentis, canalis)
**were checked by Dann against the book, 2026-09-27 00:57; all five match the draft.**
