## What the catalogue changed

- **The shape we already have is the one the evidence backs.** The frame states the fact plainly, with no hedge, and only the offer is hedged (catalogue #18). That split is what "calm authority" needs: over-hedging the fact is the "mush" warning, and hedging only the offer is not.
- **French builds its openers on the infinitive, not the gerund** (catalogue, "French notes"). So each language has its own slot form: English "letting the jaw drop", French « laisser la mâchoire descendre ». The templates hold both forms.
- **In French, « vous » plus the imperative reads less commanding than the English imperative** (catalogue, "French notes"). So « Essayez de… et observez si… » is a fair French offer, where "Try… and notice…" in English sits closer to the line. **That observation is the agent's, with no source; the desk is not sure of it.** Dann's ear decides.
- **Two warnings to build in as tests:** no word that cancels the hedge ("just", « tout simplement »), and no conditional stacked on an adverb of possibility in French (« vous pourriez peut-être »).
- **Left out:** the explicit menu (#11) belongs behind the tap, which already is a menu. The named-authority opener (#12) stays, but the catalogue found no autonomy research behind it; it is there for attribution, not for agency.

**French agreement, checked against every referent the closers can meet:** the vowel is always named alone (« le [i] », « le [u] », « le [ɛ] »), so it is masculine, and « sa couleur » and « garder » do not change with it. If a closer ever names « la voyelle » instead, « la » and anything that agrees with it turn feminine; no closer here does.

### The verb inside the offer: prefer the permissive verb

**DESK PROPOSAL, from catalogue #7 (Alexander Technique's "allow") and #6 (external focus):** where the source's own advice allows it, the action is phrased as letting something happen ("letting the jaw drop", « laisser la mâchoire descendre ») rather than making it happen ("dropping the jaw"). **This never changes what the source says** (unity): where a source says to do something actively, the offer says so too.

## What the desk is least sure of

1. **Opener 6's French**, « Voyez ce que donne… le fait de… ». It is correct and a little heavy.
2. **The mixed mood in the first French example:** « Vous pourriez essayer…, et observez… » joins a conditional to an imperative. It is common in speech, and it reads naturally to the desk, but a careful French editor may prefer « et d'observer si » (both infinitives after « essayer de »).
3. **« Bozeman suggests… ; notice whether… »** splits the offer into two sentences joined by a semicolon, because "suggests letting…, and notice…" is ungrammatical in English. The attributed opener therefore needs its own closer form, which is one more string.
