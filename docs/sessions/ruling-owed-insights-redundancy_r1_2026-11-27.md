## The shape the desk proposes, which is what he rules on

The unit stops being the bar and becomes the demand. One block per demand, at the bar where she first meets it, with
its other bars named in the heading. What differs at one bar is a clause inside the block, not a comment of its own.

> **[u] just under your secondo passaggio**
> mm. 11, 48, and 52 on D4, and m. 78 on C♯4
>
> This is what the song asks of your voice most often, four times in all. Your [u] sits just under your secondo
> passaggio and a little under its first resonance (fR1), where it tends to open a little on its own (Miller, *The
> Structure of Singing*, 1986, p. 158), and keeping it closed tends toward whoop timbre (Bozeman, *Practical Vocal
> Acoustics*, 2025, p. 94). If it suits your voice, let it lean toward [ʊ] and notice whether the [u] keeps its
> colour.
>
> To explore it: ease into the note with a slight decrescendo and see whether the [u] keeps its shape (Reid, *Voice:
> Psyche and Soma*, 1975, p. 66); or sing "you" on a quick five-note descending scale, then drop the [j] and sing
> only the [u], keeping the same shape of lips and face (Miller, *Solutions for Singers*, 2004, pp. 76 to 77).
>
> At m. 11 you arrive by leap. McKinney suggests taking it legato, thinking of the D4 as a note asking for more
> energy, space and depth rather than simply a high one (*The Diagnosis and Correction of Vocal Faults*, 1994,
> p. 189).

**The sentence the shape exists for is the first one.** "Four times in all" is a thing a singer can decide a piece
on, and today the document cannot say it, because it reports each bar as though it were the first.
