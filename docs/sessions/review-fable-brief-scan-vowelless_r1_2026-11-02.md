## 2. Rule 1: drop against fold, and the lone і/ѣ/ѵ test

**Drop is right; fold would not beat it.** The 2026-10-29 loss (V3, P 7 and B10 4, `review-opus-brief-n135-stage2b-r4_r1:30-31`) came from the folded noise text riding on a real syllable («т друг»), which the scorer's (b) then reads as wrong letters. Dropping writes nothing into `<lyric>`, so the host keeps its letters. Note count is the same either way: a folded unit keeps the host's box (`underlay-clitics.ts:12-13`), so monotone sees as many units under fold as under drop. The six losses of rule 1 are all of one kind: a misread vowel («ньъх» for «ных», «цѳн» for «цен», `fix` ledger song 4 p15 s1) leaves the unit vowelless, the unit goes, and monotone spreads its neighbours into the hole (дра n53 to n54). Fold would spread them the same way. So the two paths need not agree in mechanism; they agree in effect (no vowelless text under a note), and the report line the brief asks for ("whether the two paths now agree") can say exactly that. One of the six, song 5 p18 s3 «сп», is the truth file giving a vowelless syllable its own note: a truth-file breach of §0.1, not a reader loss; name it in the report.

**The lone-ѣ test fires on cut pieces and will drop a real syllable.** Evidence from the captures: song 3 page 1 lyrics read «ѵѣѣу» before (`work/cap/s3`) and «ѵѣу» after rule 1 (`work/capfix/s3`). Rule 1 cannot remove a letter from a token; what happened is that `cutUnits` cut the three-vowel token into pieces and the middle piece «ѣ-» had one letter, ѣ, and was dropped. The word is noise here, but «поѣду», «уѣхалъ», «пріѣхалъ» cut the same way, and their «ѣ» syllable would go. All 8 lone-і/ѣ gains in the ledger are whole one-letter tokens («І», «і», «ѣ:», «»і,»), none a piece of a cut. A whole token with one letter is what a bar line or bracket reads as; a cut piece always carries a vowel the engine placed. On a modern-spelling page the clause never fires. So: keep the clause, restrict it to a unit that is its whole token (one unit of its `origin`).
