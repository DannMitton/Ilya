## p. 175, 22.1
- Russian has two rolled consonants, written in the book as r and ɼ (the second with a hook, their symbol for the soft one; the glyph is the book's, read from the page).

## p. 176, 22.2 to 22.3
- 22.2, formation of r, four parts: (1) articulating organs, tip of the tongue against the middle of the teeth-ridge (Fig. 30); (2) state of air-passage, "alternately closed and opened a number of times at point of articulation with such rapidity that the whole process strikes the ear as forming a single sound", that is, "a very rapid succession of taps against the middle of the teeth-ridge"; (3) soft palate raised; (4) larynx, "vocal cords close together and vibrating throughout the duration of the consonant".
- Footnote 1 to 22.2: "The speaker does not make each tap consciously. All he does is to hold the tongue loosely against the teeth-ridge in such a position that when air pressure is applied, the tongue vibrates without conscious effort. The action is similar to that of a musical reed."
- 22.3: such allophones of r are voiced alveolar rolled consonants.

## p. 177, 22.4 to 22.7
- 22.4: these allophones are used at the end of words when not preceded by a voiceless consonant; the number of taps is "three or four normally". At the beginning of a word and before a consonant, "usually two taps". Between vowels, "usually only one tap", with no appreciable closure: a flap, so that allophone is strictly "a voiced alveolar flapped consonant". Footnote 1: a single tap may strike a listener whose own r is not rolled as a d (the British *very* heard as *veddy*).
- 22.5: in all positions the taps may be increased "for special 'affective' purposes"; рад with heavy emphasis "might have five or six taps", and then two or more r-symbols may be written. Footnote 2: B. V. Bratus (Leningrad University) to D. Ward: theoretically no limit to the length of Russian vowels in emphatic or affective pronunciation, and the same seems true of consonants without a stop element, at least in some positions.
- 22.6: a voiceless r, about three taps, is a subsidiary allophone, symbolized by r with a ring beneath.
- 22.7: the r of 22.2 exists in some English pronunciation, commonly in Scotland, and "is the form of r-sound generally recommended in England for use in elocution and in singing".
