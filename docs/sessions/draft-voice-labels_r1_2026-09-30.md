# Voice-type labels: a shortlist for the singer's profile

Revision 1, 2026-09-30 about 00:05. A DATED DRAFT by the desk for Dann to refine ("draft and refine; do not entrench", CONTRACT §3). Nothing reaches the tree until he rules.

## The balance rule (desk proposal)

A label enters the shortlist when it is (a) in common use in at least two of the five traditions (German Fach, Italian, French, Russian, English), or (b) central to Russian repertoire, or (c) a label singers commonly use for themselves. Everything else is free text, shown exactly as the singer typed it. The singer's chosen label is displayed; its parent routes the sources and the Bozeman band. Kloiber's ranges are role ranges for casting and are never used as limits.

## Open points for Dann

- Bass-baritone as its own parent: Miller's *Securing Baritone, Bass-Baritone, and Bass Voices* covers it; Bozeman's bands have baritone and bass buckets only. DESK DEFAULT until ruled: route to both books, and use the bass band.
- French display labels: drafted later for his ruling (French rule).
- Treble (young) voices: not in the shortlist; free text.
- Countertenors (Dann 00:01: most are baritones with excellent falsetto; some are true anomalies; "voices are so customizable"). The consistency check must not flag them: Ilya measures the vocal tract, which is an adult male tract in most countertenors, while the sung range is treble. DESK DEFAULT: for the countertenor parent, resonances are checked against the union bands (no voice-type band), and range against the treble sources. Bozeman classes the countertenor as a treble voice for singing strategy (KVP2, Definitions, p. 145, via the project's KVP2 synthesis).
