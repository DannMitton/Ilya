**Before you begin.** Please tell me a little about yourself in this: teacher, coach, or singer (or all three), your voice type, and whether you used a phone or a computer. Set aside about an hour, with one song you know well.

1. **Can you find your way without me?** Before you open the Guide, take your song from score to transcription. Where did you hesitate, hunt, or get stuck? I'd rather hear about the clumsy moments than the smooth ones.
2. **Did Ilya read your score faithfully?** Where do Ilya's notes or words differ from your copy, and how easily could you set them right?
3. **Would you rely on it?** Find one place where Ilya's IPA or Insights differs from what you'd teach. Which bar, what does Ilya say, and what would you say instead? If you find none, tell me what you checked.
4. **Is it a pleasure to use?** What, if anything, would make you want to come back to it, or keep you away?
5. **Is Ilya useful to you?** Tell me what you'd actually reach for it to do this month (in your studio, your coaching, your own practice), and what you'd leave it out of.
6. **Would you recommend it?** To whom, and what would you warn them about? Be as candid as you'd want a colleague to be with you.
7. **What didn't I ask that I should have?**

## The logic, one line each (the desk's)

- The opening note gives the context every answer needs (voice, role, device) and makes the answers comparable.
- 1 comes first, while the experience is unguided. 2 follows while the score is in front of them; it tests THE ONE THING. 3 moves from what Ilya read to what Ilya concludes; the bar forces a specific answer and the exit stops an invented disagreement. 4 bridges doing and judging. 5 asks need late, so it gets a judgement, not a guess. 6 is the verdict; "to whom" and "warn" make it specific. 7 catches what none of us thought to ask.
- Voice rules applied (memory, scholarly voice): no absolutes, no agency given to constructs (questions 2 and 3 reworded 20:4x).
