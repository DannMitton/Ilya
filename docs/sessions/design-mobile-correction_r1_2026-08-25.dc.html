The loupe sits on its own measure. The page does not slide out from under it.

A single tap near the fault raises the loupe on that measure, magnified about 2.4 times in place, with the tapped entry taken. Single tap, not double tap, so the double-tap zoom habit keeps its meaning.

The page beneath stays whole and stays itself. Its systems run behind the loupe at page zoom and reappear at both margins, and system 3 is still where it was.

Two marks answer the question of place, and they do different jobs. The measure tag in the loupe's top left names what the loupe holds: m. 14 · system 3 of 6. The place rail at the right edge shows which slice of the whole page the window is looking at, with one lavender tick for the loupe's measure. The readout names the entry, not the measure, so nothing is said twice.

The insertion bar and the horizontal pitch crossbar are Finale's Speedy grammar, unaltered. The bar stands at the taken entry, the crossbar states its pitch, and the stepper walks the bar across barlines into the next measure, which carries the loupe with it.

The stepper straddles the readout it moves, which saves a whole station of height and puts navigation where the reader is already looking. The named Undo sits alone on the row above, so it can grow to fit its sentence without crowding anything.

Station order runs DURATION, PITCH, ACCIDENTAL · ENTRY, LYRIC. Durations lead because N.95 measured them as the broken channel, 0 of 28 confident before re-derivation.

Every control on the surface is 44 px tall and at least 44 px wide, measurable on this drawing because it is at true scale. The page's own glyphs stay small, which item 9 of the ruled ground exempts.

The drawer's pull is still on the left edge of the page, unmoved. The surface is a sibling dock, not the drawer slimmed, which is the §6 answer to your sixth question.

Tapping tuplet swaps the DURATION station's row in place for the two tiers. The label gains a back chevron, and the station's neighbours stay put. Nothing opens over anything, so no modal face appears and ruling 4 holds.

The quick tier is the counts, triplet first, then 5, 6, 7, then 2, 4, 8. That is Speedy's Option+2 to 8 set reordered by how often a singer needs it, rather than by numeric order. A tap applies the tuplet at once, with no confirm.

The full tier is the Tuplet Definition's rhythmic half, written as the sentence it is: 3 of ♪ in the space of 2 of ♪. Each of the four slots steps on tap, the numbers through 2 to 9 and the values through the five durations. Stepping is slower than a picker for a distant value, and it is the price of keeping every control on one surface. The last definition stays as the default, per ruling 11.

Your fourth question asks whether the lyric row dims in a gap. The proposal is that it does not dim. It says what is wrong, in words, and the greying follows the words rather than carrying the meaning alone.

A bar in a gap has no syllable to shift, because the note that would carry the syllable does not exist yet. So the LYRIC label reads LYRIC · TAKE A NOTE TO SHIFT ITS SYLLABLE, which is the instruction rather than a diagnosis. Delete and Tie go the same way for the same reason. Principle 8 is satisfied because the sentence carries the state and the colour only agrees with it.

The readout stops naming a note and names the place instead: gap after на · the next duration enters here. The DURATION row stays fully lit, because in a gap it is the one station that acts.

The PITCH label carries the answer to your third question. A note entered here arrives at the pitch of the previous entry, so the label reads PITCH · TAKES THE PITCH OF на before the note exists, and the pitch verbs finish it. Where there is no previous entry in the part, the note arrives on the staff's middle line. That last clause is mine, and it is geometry, not a musical guess.

A rest enters the same way: take a duration with the bar in a gap and no pitch, and a rest of that duration appears. That is Speedy's rule, and it is why Rest stays lit here.

Rotation is the mode switch, so the dock answers rotation rather than width. In landscape it moves to the left edge, which is the drawer's own ruled anchor, and the chevron turns to point left because it still points the way the dock moves. The stations keep their order, their labels, their verb strings, and their 44 px floor. Nothing is dropped and nothing is renamed, so there is nothing to re-learn on the way round.

The dock is 380 px wide because 356 px of content is the narrowest column that holds the seven-cell DURATION row at 44 px each without wrapping to a second line. A narrower dock would wrap two stations and push the total past 430 px of height.

The page gets 552 px of width, which shows a full system at readable zoom. That is the natural exploration your second ruling describes, and portrait loses nothing by comparison because the loupe already gave the singer the measure at working size.

Reach is the untested part. A left dock in landscape sits under the left thumb, and no coarse-pointer measurement supports or refutes that yet. This is the same limit the brief records: everything measured this session ran in desktop Chrome emulation.

The collision the brief names is resolved by moving the raise onto a single tap. Double tap keeps the platform's zoom, single tap raises the loupe, and the two never compete for the same finger. That works because tap already means take a note in the shipped app, and the loupe is only that selection made large enough to work on.

Press and hold stays empty on the page and on the loupe on purpose. The platform trains it for text selection and for context menus, and neither belongs here. Leaving it unassigned costs nothing and keeps the grammar free of a gesture the singer would trigger by accident while reading.

One addition needs stating plainly, because it is mine. When the loupe rises on a measure near the bottom of the page window, the page pans by the smallest amount that clears the surface, so the loupe never opens underneath the dock. The memo records that auto-pan behaviour is undocumented for nearly every app surveyed, so there is no precedent to adopt here.

§6 · YOUR SEVEN OPEN QUESTIONS, PROPOSED ON
1
The tap grammar as one table

The §5 table. Single tap raises the loupe, double tap keeps the platform's zoom, press and hold stays reserved, drag pans, pinch resizes the page only, swipe down dismisses the surface. No cell contends with another.

2
The tuplet count row and the full form

Both tiers live inside the DURATION station, disclosed in place by the tuplet cell. The quick tier is the counts with 3 first. The full tier is the definition written as a sentence with four stepping slots. No modal face, and the placement follows from tuplets being duration work.

3
The pitch of a freshly entered note

Your desk's proposal, adopted: the note arrives at the previous entry's pitch and the pitch verbs finish it. The PITCH label says so before the note exists, which turns the rule into recognition instead of recall. Where the part has no previous entry, the note arrives on the staff's middle line.

4
Whether the lyric row dims in a gap

It does not dim on its own. The label states the condition as an instruction, and the greying agrees with the words rather than carrying them. Principle 8 forbids the dim alone. Delete and Tie follow the same rule for the same reason.

5
The Undo affordance's exact form

A full-round pill on its own header row, reading ↰ Undo: quarter → eighth: the change stated in the direction it happened, so the pill names what it reverses. It sits alone on that row so it can grow to fit a long sentence. With nothing to undo, the pill is absent rather than disabled, because a control that cannot act earns no ink.

6
Where the surface lives relative to the drawer

A sibling dock, not the drawer slimmed. The drawer is a side-entering object with anchors ruled in E.36 §1.4, and re-anchoring it to the bottom edge in portrait would move those anchors and make geometry answer width. The dock keeps the drawer's grammar, one bare pull pointing the way it moves, the same station labels, the same three radii, the same 180 ms. Only one of the two is open at a time: raising the loupe closes the drawer, and opening the drawer sends the dock away. The bottom-docked position is the one living precedent the memo found, in Soundslice and, on your own account, Newzik.

7
How the loupe announces which measure it holds

Two marks with two jobs. The measure tag inside the loupe's top left names the measure and its system: m. 14 · system 3 of 6. The place rail at the page's right edge shows which slice of the whole page is in the window, with one tick for the loupe's measure. The readout keeps to naming the entry, so the measure is stated once.

§8 · ONE MATTER FOR YOUR RULING

The loupe and the operational spec disagree, and I cannot settle it myself.

Your ruling of 2026-08-25 puts the loupe over the whole page, shadowed, nearest the user. The Calm Authority operational spec of 2026-08-18 says nothing floats over the paper, and the filed copy of that ruling states that the eleven principles and the operational spec stand unamended. The later date governs the loupe, so I drew the loupe. But the shape rule is still on the books, and it is load-bearing for everything else that might one day want to float.

Two readings are open. Either the loupe is a named exception and the shape rule gains one clause, or the loupe is not a floating thing at all: the region of paper it covers is replaced rather than overlaid, which the motion rule already describes for the paper, and the shadow goes. The second reading changes the drawing.

Does ruling 3 amend the shape rule with a named exception for the loupe, or is the loupe a replacement of the paper it covers, with no shadow?
