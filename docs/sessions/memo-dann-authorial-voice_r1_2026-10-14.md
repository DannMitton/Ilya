## His two registers

**Published register** (thesis 2020, JOS 2022, VoicePrints 2021). Formal, attributive, measured. Long sentences built from series and subordinate clauses, cut by short declaratives at turning points. Third person for the singer ("the LMV", "the singer"), first person singular for his own method and experience, first person plural for the profession ("reminds us", "we develop"). Citation by footnote, with the source's author named inside the sentence and often given a role.

**Conversational register** (italic quotations in the project records). Fast, warm, frank, and exploratory. Questions ending a thought ("Critique.", "right?"), hedges of memory ("IIRC", "I think?"), humour ("lol", "(duh)", "Singers are weird."), strong preference language ("God yes", "I absolutely reject", "I hate"). Uncorrected typing. Second person addressed to the desk.

**Which suits Insights.** Neither alone. The records rule a blend, and the blend is specific:

- From the published register: the attribution habits, the hedging that matches the evidence, the field vocabulary, the paired critique-and-remedy, the restraint.
- From the conversational register and his rulings: warmth, the singer's agency, offers in place of commands, and plain words. His target words: *"friendly, Calm Authority. Approchable, critiquable."* (`PRODUCT.md:247`, 2026-09-23 17:28) and *"Calm Authority: Helpful, useful, and unassailably tethered to the literature it is drawn from."* (`OPEN.md:489`, 2026-09-28 23:17).
- Not from either: second person addressed to the singer ("your voice", "the *secondo passaggio* you gave"). His published prose speaks of "the LMV" in the third person (`thesis2020.txt:4465-4466`); Insights' second person comes from rulings (`OPEN.md:493`, `PRODUCT.md:1186`), not from his scholarly habit. A writer who imitates the thesis will get the person wrong.

Where his 2020 to 2022 published prose and his 2026 rulings conflict (the word "because", absolutes, contrastive "not" constructions), the 2026 rulings govern Insights. Each conflict is noted under its rule.

## The rules

Each rule states a habit, gives verbatim examples with locations, and says how it applies to Insights.

### A. Sentence shape

**A1. Long, layered sentences, cut by a short declarative at a turning point.** His published median sentence runs about 25 words (rough count, see "What I could not establish"); the long ones carry series and qualifications, and a short one lands the point.
- "We are getting older." (`jos2022.txt:13`, the article's opening)
- "The task of the voice teacher is to detect functional challenges such as those named above, advise referrals to healthcare professionals when necessary (e.g., laryngologist), and otherwise devise interventions to support the singer. That begins with listening." (`jos2022.txt:64-68`)
- "This is the long game." (`jos2022.txt:161`)
- Insights: one or two medium sentences per idea; a short sentence is allowed where it carries a fact. A short sentence whose only work is to sound like a realization is banned (`CONTRACT.md:102`, his words: *"AI slop masquerading as an epiphany"*).

**A2. Series in threes, with the Oxford comma; stacked verbs as precision.** He lists actions and qualities in parallel series and treats the stack as deliberate.
- "detect ..., advise referrals ..., and otherwise devise interventions" (`jos2022.txt:64-67`)
- "help the user locate, learn, and negotiate Russian vocal repertoire" (`PRODUCT.md:297`, his words 2026-09-24)
- "the singer's goal is to perform confidently, expressively, and sustainably" (`PRODUCT.md:233`; "sustainably" his, `PRODUCT.md:235`)
- Insights: a stack is welcome where each verb names a distinct act. Do not trim his stacks when editing his prose (his tie-breaker in the account preferences).

**A3. Parentheticals carry the precise term, the example, or the aside.** Parentheses hold a technical gloss, an e.g., a source detail, or a warm aside, without breaking the line of thought.
- "(listening, interventions, and resources)" (`jos2022.txt:24`)
- "a supporter who was (and is!) deeply invested in protecting his enthusiasm for singing" (`jos2022.txt:293-295`)
- "fR2 (which does not figure into the calculations here, but was useful in offering more information about the vowels [ɑ] and [ʌ], which share an fR1 value)" (`thesis2020.txt:3841-3843`)
- Insights: one parenthetical per sentence at most; use it for the IPA symbol, the pitch, or the field term. The "(and is!)" kind of aside is his warmth in prose; in Insights it collides with the no-exclamation house rule, so keep warmth in word choice instead.

**A4. Section pivots by a question (published only).**
- "Can one recapture youthful vigor?" (`jos2022.txt:81`)
- "When does aging begin for the singer?" (`jos2022.txt:120`)
- Insights: do not use rhetorical questions. The "why should I care?" question is the desk's test and is never printed (`PRODUCT.md:312`, his words: *"Let's not be so transparent as to allow 'Why should you care' into user-facing communication"*). The question-like closer "and notice whether ..." is the ruled form (`PRODUCT.md:1033-1034`).

### B. Hedging and asserting

**B1. Hedge to the strength of the evidence; assert flatly only a definition or a settled relation.** Modal verbs (may, might, could), "seems", "appears", "perhaps", "likely", "tends to". Definitions and physical relations are stated without a hedge.
- Hedged: "It is possible that my vocal tract resonates a little higher than Bozeman reports. ... Other individuals' values will vary." (`thesis2020.txt:3875-3878`)
- Hedged: "Based on the data analyzed here, it would seem that the LMV's ongoing acoustic concerns deal mainly with ..." (`thesis2020.txt:5046-5048`)
- Asserted: "Noise is nonperiodic (nonharmonic) information in the sound signal." (`jos2022.txt:263`)
- Asserted: "Expert listening is vital to teaching voice." (`jos2022.txt:72`)
- Insights: the ruled three strengths match this (`PRODUCT.md:263`): a universal relation applied to the singer's own measured values may be stated; a trend whose size varies is stated as a direction ("tends to"); where neither exists, nothing is said. The ruled consequence sentence: "what the voice tends to do on its own; but what resisting it tends to bring" (`OPEN.md:489`).

**B2. Owned inference: when he reasons past his data, he says so and marks it as his.** He uses "This may be because ...", "I feel", "More study is needed".
- "It may be easier for the LMV to modify the climactic [o] vowel in mm. 69-70 toward [ɑ] (Fig. 6.71). This may be because mid-close/mid-open [o] can be challenging to negotiate in the extreme upper register ..." (`thesis2020.txt:4669-4672`)
- "This may be simply the composers' intuition at work. More study is needed." (`thesis2020.txt:4975`)
- "In the context of this thesis, I feel that this kind of comparison would suggest that singing in Russian is better or worse than singing in another language." (`thesis2020.txt:807-808`)
- Insights: a connection that reasons past what any source states shows its steps and is vetted by Dann once (`PRODUCT.md:211`, `:233`). On the page, a line never presents that step as the source's claim.

**B3. Concede, then hold the position.** "Admittedly", "Despite", "Like all ..., however", "While ...".
- "Admittedly, swallowing is not singing. But these muscles of the pharyngeal wall have not been convincingly discounted as agents of a widening pharynx during the act of singing. Therefore, this claim may be something to reconsider." (`vp.txt:778-784`)
- "Like all voice pedagogy resources in our constantly evolving discipline, this book contains some dated information. However, it falls squarely on the side of must-have resources ..." (`vp.txt:785-789`)
- "Admittedly, sung Russian requires specific training for teachers and coaches who are not familiar with its intricacies." (`thesis2020.txt:5146-5147`)
- Insights: where sources differ, say so and keep both (`PRODUCT.md:245`: "Where sources differ, Insights says so."). A concession clause ("While ...") followed by the offer is a natural Insights shape.

**B4. Scope statements: he says what he is not claiming.**
- "It is not to suggest that sung Russian is a panacea that will guarantee well-coordinated, technically secure singing." (`thesis2020.txt:796-797`)
- "An in-depth discussion of serious age-related diseases ... is outside the scope of this article." (`jos2022.txt:28-30`)
- Insights: the ruled teacher lines do this work ("Ilya does not invent advice. ... Check any approach it suggests with your teacher.", `OPEN.md:2951-2953`, line B). Individual comments do not repeat scope statements.

**B5. Absolutes: present in the published register, rejected for Insights.** His 2020 prose uses absolutes where his data supported them; his 2026 rulings reject edicts and absolute declarations.
- Published: "None include the degree of nuance that Grayson's work explains methodically." (`thesis2020.txt:752-753`)
- Published: "Every other fR1 lies beyond the singable pitch range." (`thesis2020.txt:1979-1980`)
- 2026: *"when I read assertions like 'an inserted word takes open notes only' I feel squirmy because there will always be a condition that justifiably lives outside of that edict."* (`CONTRACT-archive.md:353-355`); *"this is why I hate these absolute edicts as rulings."* (`PRODUCT.md:131`)
- Insights: no every, none, never, always, all about the singer or the voice. Bound the claim instead (my term, **bounded claim**): "in most voices", "in the range of this piece", "for the vowels Bozeman charts". His own "in the LMV range studied in this thesis (A2-E4)" (`thesis2020.txt:1977-1978`) is the model: the absolute is fenced by its range.

**B6. "Because": used freely in the published register; in Insights only where the source states the cause.** The thesis and JOS use "because" often (about 32 instances in the thesis body including quotations; 4 in JOS). The rule against it in Insights comes from the caller's brief; I did not find his wording of it in the corpus (see "What I could not establish").
- Published, a cause he owns: "Because palatalization imposes tongue fronting, I was also curious about vowel-adjacent fronting agents" (`thesis2020.txt:3591-3593`)
- Published, a hedged cause: "This may be because the composer sets his longest rhythmic durations on [o] rather than [i]." (Chapter 6, `thesis2020.txt` near 4194-4196)
- Insights: link facts with "and", "as", "where", "so", or a colon, unless the cited page states the cause. A cause the desk infers is marked as inference behind the tap, never printed as the source's claim.

### C. Attribution inside the sentence

**C1. The source's author is the grammatical subject, with an attribution verb chosen for what the author does.** He varies the verb with care: reports, writes, suggests, identifies, asserts, recounts, recalls, defines, calls, explains, adds, offers, points out, demystifies, advises.
- "Williamson reports that 'the fastest growing segment of the population is in the over age 85 category.'" (`jos2022.txt:18-19`)
- "Pragmatic Miller identifies the cause of a wobble as insufficient resistance to airflow, and suggests that appoggio is the solution." (`jos2022.txt:258-260`)
- "Gill and Herbst call this skill building process 'voice building,' and assert unequivocally that it is 'the most central area of voice pedagogy.'" (`jos2022.txt:139-141`)
- "Doscher explains, ... She adds, ..." (`thesis2020.txt:1900-1903`)
- Insights: the ruled attributed opener "[Author] suggests letting ..." puts the advice in the source's mouth (`PRODUCT.md:1032`). Match the verb to the source: "describes" for a description, "suggests" for advice, "reports" for a finding. Do not upgrade a source's "may" to "shows".

**C2. Role appositive: he names what the source is before the name.** (Term mine.)
- "Gerontologist Dr. Tracey Gendron questions the ethics of feeding the larger narrative of aging as a progressive decline." (`jos2022.txt:70-72`)
- "Voice researcher Sangeetha Rayapati writes about class voice and the aging singer." (`jos2022.txt:150-151`)
- "SLP Kerrie Obert (who contributed Figure 7-lI on p. 200), points out that ..." (`vp.txt:773-774`)
- Insights: rarely needed, as the short citation names the source. Useful in a "Notice this" line, where a role tells the singer why the source is worth hearing.

**C3. He credits coinages and names terms with their author.**
- "Bozeman has called this passive modification option vowel migration." (`thesis2020.txt:1908-1909`)
- "semi-occluded vocal tract exercises (abbreviated SOVTs: a term coined by Ingo Titze)" (`thesis2020.txt:3079-3080`)
- "Janice Chapman's self-styled 'Dial-a-Vowel' exercise" (`jos2022.txt:253`)
- Insights: a field term keeps its owner and citation ("Field terms such as Bozeman's 'turns' stay, with their citation", `PRODUCT.md:1047`).

**C4. Quotation is load-bearing, and the citation names a page.** He quotes the source's words where the exact words matter and footnotes with a page. His 2026 rule extends this to every Insights comment.
- "Advocacy for these kinesthetic singing tools comes with the sensible advice that 'once long-term skill acquisition is achieved, the tools need only be used as an infrequent reset.' (p. 5)" (`vp.txt:728-731`)
- "Each chapter concludes with a much-appreciated Selected Resources section that effectively leaves a metaphorical trail of citation breadcrumbs for further reading." (`vp.txt:714-716`)
- 2026: *"leaving a trail of breadcrumbs for users to follow back to the primary sources... It's not cute to make these people work harder to locate and verify our claims on their own."* (`PRODUCT.md:1040`)
- Insights: every comment carries "(Author, *Short title*, Year, p. N)" (`PRODUCT.md:1042`). No source, no advice (`PRODUCT.md:326`).

### D. Connectives

**D1. Contrast and addition connectives, placed at the head of the sentence.** By contrast, Conversely, Likewise, In addition, Further, Therefore, However, While, Despite, In other words, Again.
- "By contrast, lyric diction coaches use IPA notation prescriptively ..." (`thesis2020.txt:831-833`)
- "Conversely, the traditional classical objective of the gola aperta or 'open throat' seems to be facilitated by the fronted version of the Russian basis of articulation ..." (`thesis2020.txt:5061-5063`)
- "In other words, we really only notice tremolos and wobbles outside the perceived norm of 5–7 cycles per second." (`jos2022.txt:251-253`)
- Insights: "While" and "Where" suit the hazard-and-help pair; "but" is ruled in for the consequence sentence (`OPEN.md:493`).

**D2. Contrastive "not" constructions: in his published prose; banned in Insights.**
- Published, his own: "equipping aging singers with functional tools to unlock their vocal potential in the present, not the past." (`jos2022.txt:167-169`)
- 2026: banned, "'it is not X, it is Y' for rhythm" (`CONTRACT.md:102`); the brief extends this to "not this but that" constructions.
- Insights: state the positive claim alone. Where a contrast is the fact (two vowels on one pitch), name both as facts in their own clauses.

### E. Vocabulary

**E1. Favoured verbs:** curate, negotiate, navigate, equip, facilitate, promote, inform, demystify.
- "The purpose of this article is to curate some thoughts on voice pedagogy" (`jos2022.txt:23`)
- "how well the singer is likely to negotiate a Russian piece as written" (`PRODUCT.md:211`, his words)
- "To navigate these technical demands, the LMV may open [i] slightly to [ɪ]" (`thesis2020.txt:4465-4466`)
- "Considering these cycles ... equips teachers with another way to inform appropriate LMV repertoire selection." (`thesis2020.txt:86-88`)

**E2. Favoured nouns and adjectives:** informed (teacher, pedagogue, ear, instruction), coordination (well-coordinated; optimal and less optimal), demands, hallmarks, stakeholders, nuance, bespoke, latent, germane, "falls squarely".
- "the informed voice teacher who understands and can address the needs of the aging singer" (`jos2022.txt:20-21`)
- "A fronted tongue and a low stable larynx are two vocal tract configurations known to be hallmarks of well-coordinated classical singing." (`thesis2020.txt:708-709`)
- *"There are no faults, just optimal and less optimal coordination"* (`PRODUCT.md:271`)

**E3. Words he rules out for Insights.** "fault", "flaw", "diagnose", "diagnosis" (`PRODUCT.md:271-273`); "try" in what Insights says (`OPEN.md:493`, 2026-09-29, which supersedes the "try" openers of 2026-09-25 at `PRODUCT.md:1032`); "hold" for duration, use sustain, prolong, or extend (`OPEN.md:502`); "modification" and "migration" in the visible line (`OPEN.md:499`); "meets" in the summary line, use "sits close to" (`PRODUCT.md:1180`); "your voice type", use "your voice" (`PRODUCT.md:1190`); bare "primo" or "secondo", use the full terms in italics (`PRODUCT.md:459`, `OPEN.md:3016`); percentages, use plain fractions such as "about a third" (`OPEN.md:3035`); moral words such as "wrong" (`CONTRACT.md:103`).

### F. Address and stance toward the reader

**F1. Published: first person singular for his own work and experience; first person plural for the profession; "the reader" for direct invitation.**
- "In my DMA practicum I was assigned a tenor in his late 70s ..." (`jos2022.txt:276-279`)
- "Cornelius Reid's seminal essay ... reminds us to listen for function first." (`jos2022.txt:80-86`)
- "The reader is encouraged to read Chapman's description in her own words" (`jos2022.txt:282-284`); "The reader is invited to inspect the sound files" (`thesis2020.txt:3889-3890`)
- Insights: second person to the singer, by ruling. Ilya speaks of itself in the third person ("Ilya does not invent advice", `OPEN.md:2953`). No "I" and no "we" in a comment.

**F2. Imperatives addressed to teachers in JOS; offers addressed to singers in Insights.**
- JOS: "Reject compensations. Propose the intervention, set it up well, and sing. Accept what comes ..." (`jos2022.txt:157-158`); "Consult a certified speech and language therapist" (`jos2022.txt:217-218`)
- 2026: *"We want to avoid declaring pedagogical interventions as a certainty even if they are grounded in the literature, and always prioritize the user's agency."* (`PRODUCT.md:1029`)
- Insights: "You might let ...", "If it suits your voice, let ...", "Consider letting ...", "[Author] suggests letting ..." (`OPEN.md:493`, `PRODUCT.md:1032`); outcomes as possibilities ("which can help"), never promises; "and notice whether ..." where the singer can hear or feel the result (`PRODUCT.md:1033-1034`).

**F3. Deference to the informed ear and to medicine.**
- "voice teachers understand and embrace their role as part of a greater voice care team that will include certified medical experts to whom one defers whenever issues of health and singing intersect." (`jos2022.txt:30-33`)
- "If unwanted noise persists, advise the student to get scoped." (`jos2022.txt:270-271`)
- *"The informed ear of a teacher should be the decising factor, and obviously that lies beyond Ilya's capabilities."* (`PRODUCT.md:324`)
- Insights: "Check this approach with your teacher." on each comment that suggests an approach (`OPEN.md:2953`, line A, Dann's words).

### G. Warmth and humour

**G1. Warmth through respect for the person, not through praise words.**
- "I decided that the best way to honor his keen intellect and desire to sing in our short six weeks together, was to ignore the pervasive rattle in his sound." (`jos2022.txt:287-292`)
- "Male perspectives on the singing voice dominate our discipline, yet here women's expertise is amplified." (`vp.txt:709-711`)
- *"Some singers seize challenges eagerly, while others choose to capitalize on their own current strengths whil;e they accrue more nuanced coordination. Both are valid appraoches, and we endorse neither."* (`OPEN.md:2947`)

**G2. Humour is dry, brief, and kept out of technical claims.**
- "Reminding this potentially dull boy to play is trickier." (`thesis2020.txt:102`)
- Bio: "two giant, fluffy, ridiculous Bernese Mountain Dogs. While they haven't quite mastered barking in harmony yet, it is a musical family, so it is only a matter of time." (`jos2022.txt:452-454`; third person, authorship not established)
- Conversational: *"Singers are weird."* (`PRODUCT.md:1077`); *"they are there to gather information and insights (duh)"* (`OPEN.md:2936`)
- Insights: no jokes. Warmth comes from "If it suits your voice", from naming what the music allows, and from never treating expression as a fault (`PRODUCT.md:328`).

### H. Technical terms

**H1. Introduce a term by apposition or "also known as", then use it consistently.**
- "The loss of muscle mass from aging is also known as sarcopenia." (`jos2022.txt:53`)
- "psychoacoustics, that branch of psychology concerned with perception of sound" (`jos2022.txt:75-78`)
- "When the sung pitch (which we will equate here with fo, or the frequency of oscillation) occurs an octave or more below the fR1 of the vowel being sung, it is said to be sung in 'open timbre.'" (`thesis2020.txt:922-924`)

**H2. Name the competing terms, then choose one with a reason.**
- "Cover has also been called 'coperto,' 'protection,' 'vowel modification,' and 'turning over.'" (`thesis2020.txt:1898-1899`)
- "'Whoop timbre,' or perhaps more fittingly 'whoop coupling'" (`thesis2020.txt:1952`)
- "This book joins the majority of recent voice pedagogy resources in perpetuating the use of the word 'formant' as a shorthand for 'resonance of the vocal tract,' when in fact the two are not strict synonyms." (`vp.txt:758-761`)
- Insights: the gloss lives behind the tap or in Learn; the visible line uses the field term with its citation and is not glossed for a hypothetical other reader (`PRODUCT.md:1047`). The register (plain, working, technical) follows the singer's declared fluency (`PRODUCT.md:1053`).

### I. Hazard together with what helps

**I1. Every hazard arrives with a way forward; a critique arrives with a better alternative.** (Term mine: **hazard-and-help pair**.)
- Thesis: "The close vowel [i] is approached by leap while executing a crescendo. To navigate these technical demands, the LMV may open [i] slightly to [ɪ], moving the fR1 of [i] up and out of the way of the sung pitch." (`thesis2020.txt:4464-4466`)
- JOS: "If noise in the sung tone is detected, try some exploratory vocalises (e.g., gentle sustained notes, glides, and staccati) to determine whether that noise is a momentary artifact ... If unwanted noise persists, advise the student to get scoped." (`jos2022.txt:264-271`)
- VoicePrints, critique with remedy: "Chapter Seven's discussion of Resonance could be meaningfully enhanced by paraphrasing Miller or Bozeman's near-ubiquitous work on the interactions between harmonics and resonances of the vocal tract, rather than just referencing it in a callout box." (`vp.txt:753-757`)
- 2026: *"I really don't like saying no or just pointing our seemingly insurmountable problems without also offering solutions."* (`OPEN.md:2940`); ruled: "A hazard is forecast together with what helps; an opportunity is named together with what it allows." (`PRODUCT.md:245`)

**I2. The model sentence names an opportunity in the music.** *"This leap can be exploited to camouflage the timbral differences between these two pitch/vowel combinations."* (`PRODUCT.md:245`, `:326`, his sentence of 2026-09-23 00:43). Note its shape: the music is the subject; the modal "can" sets an offer; the verb ("exploited", "camouflage") is concrete and a little playful; the object is technical and exact.

**I3. A verdict never stands alone.** *"the closest we whould let Ilya get to saying flat-out NO to a piece for the user is to maybe say something like 'considering x, y, and z, this song seems unlikely to fit your voice well.'"* (`OPEN.md:2940`), ratified with "always followed by a way forward" (`OPEN.md:2951`). Ratified French of such a sentence: `OPEN.md:2967`.

### J. What he never does (in the corpus read)

- No exclamation marks in his published body text except inside a parenthetical aside ("(and is!)", `jos2022.txt:294`) and inside a quotation (Howell's "Gently(!!)", `jos2022.txt:217`).
- No hype or billing of a finding (`CONTRACT.md:102`).
- No moral judgement of a singer or a page ("fault", "wrong"; `PRODUCT.md:271`, `CONTRACT.md:103`). He critiques books, never singers: even the harshest appendix entries judge the notation ("Challis incorrectly uses a cedilla", `thesis2020.txt:5661`) and give the better form ("A more accurate singing transcription for this word would be [ɡlʲɑ.ˈʒu].", `thesis2020.txt:5667-5668`).
- No unsourced claim about the voice in Insights (*"We do not want one baseless claim in Ilya."*, `PRODUCT.md:326`).
- No telling the singer what their own voice is doing, or that their sense of it is wrong (desk proposal, unruled, `PRODUCT.md:1079`; consistent with his *"Ilya does not exist to argue or antagonize such singers"*, same line).
- No lesson in place of the insight (*"people are coming to Insights for insights! Not for a pedagogy or acoustic lesson"*, `PRODUCT.md:249`).
- No data dump (*"These purposes are undermined when we data dump."*, `PRODUCT.md:297`).

## A finding about sample 1's premise, from his own thesis

The brief's first example, a bass voice "whose sung pitch meets the first resonance of the vowel [ɑ] on a sustained high note", conflicts with Dann's published data. His measured fR1 for [ɑ] is 617 Hz, about D#5 (`thesis2020.txt:3829-3830`), and he writes that the fo-meets-fR1 configuration "is acoustically impossible on any other vowel [than [i]] in the LMV range studied in this thesis (A2-E4)" (`thesis2020.txt:1976-1979`), with [ɑ]'s point of turning "near the apex of the LMV's singable range in 'chest'" (Chapter 6 discussion, near `thesis2020.txt:4968-4969`). The event a bass meets on a high sustained [ɑ] is the turn, where the second harmonic passes fR1, an octave below (`thesis2020.txt:928-931`, `:5046-5049`). I wrote sample 1 about that event. If Dann intends a different voice or vowel, the sample changes; that is his call.
