### The geometry

- `:1476-1508` replaces the abutting cluster. The block is:

```ts
const gapWanted = sp(COURTESY_GAP_SP);
const rightEdge = nx - headHalfW - 1.5;
const floor = newMeasure ? nx - 16 : -Infinity;
const overrun = Math.max(0, floor - (rightEdge - (wL + wA + wR + 2 * gapWanted)));
const gap = Math.max(0, gapWanted - overrun / 2);
const clusterW = wL + wA + wR + 2 * gap;
const gx = Math.max(rightEdge - clusterW, floor);
```

`overrun` is how far a full-gap cluster would reach past the measure-opening
floor, and zero when it does not reach past it at all. Each side gives back half
of it, which buys back exactly the overrun, so `rightEdge - clusterW` lands on
the floor and the right edge does not move. When `overrun` exceeds `2 *
gapWanted`, `gap` clamps to 0, `clusterW` is the bare sum, and the two lines are
increment 1's arithmetic character for character. **A cluster too wide for its
bar therefore gets exactly what Dann walked on 2026-09-02**, and loses nothing
it had.

The gap goes half on each side rather than closing one side first. The brackets
stay symmetrical about the accidental at every width, and an asymmetric pair
reads as a mistake rather than as tight spacing. JUDGEMENT.

## 2. The three cases, and how each test reaches its own

Each test changes only the glyph widths the font reports. The rule is never
special-cased, and no test asserts a hardcoded coordinate without deriving it
from `COURTESY_GAP_SP` and the font's own widths.

| case | font | where | overrun | gap each side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the floor does not bind | `syntheticSmuflFont`, 1.18 sp | mid-measure, so no floor at all | none | 2.4 px, the full value |
| it binds by less than two gaps | `fontOfWidth(0.3)` | measure opening | 2.9 px | 0.95 px |
| it binds by more | `syntheticSmuflFont`, 1.18 sp | measure opening | 39.86 px | 0 |

The middle case needs a font of its own. At the default 12 px `lineGap` the
1.18-space synthetic font overruns the floor by 39.86 px, which two gaps of 2.4
cannot buy back, so it can only ever reach the third case. `fontOfWidth` gives
every glyph the width it is handed, which puts the case a test lands in under one
number.

The middle case asserts four things together, and the third is the one that
matters: the gap is strictly between 0 and the full value, the two sides are
still equal, the cluster's left edge sits exactly on the floor, and **the
cluster's right edge is exactly where a bare accidental's right edge would be**.
That last assertion is the ruling itself. If the gap ever gave back more or less
than the overrun, the right edge would move and the accidental would leave the
place a required one would have taken.

## 4. What the gap does at the tightest place the rule can reach

At the production stave (`lineGap: 5.5`) in Finale Maestro, on a measure-opening
note, with the notehead half-width at 3.498 units and the barline's ink ending
1.725 units left of the `nx - 16` floor. Only three rows can occur, for the
reason `memo-n102-courtesy-accidentals_r1_2026-09-02.md` §2 gives: a courtesy
carries only a flat, a natural, or a sharp.

| accidental | full-gap left edge | floor binds | gap each side | clearance to barline ink |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| natural | `nx - 14.986` | no | 1.100, full | 2.739 |
| flat | `nx - 15.976` | no, by 0.024 | 1.100, full | 1.749 |
| sharp | `nx - 16.592` | **yes, by 0.592** | 0.804 | 1.725 |

**A measure-opening courtesy sharp is the middle case at production size**, and
it gives back 0.296 units on each side, keeping 0.146 stave-spaces of the 0.2
ruled. **Nothing on this document is that case**, so it is arithmetic and not a
reading: bar 3's courtesy is a natural at a measure opening, and bar 6's sharp is
mid-measure.

The narrowest the rule can now produce anywhere at production size is **1.725
units**, for that sharp, where the floor holds it. The floor's whole job is to
keep the cluster off the barline, so this number cannot go below 1.725 however
wide the accidental gets. It was 3.333 before the gap.

A flat at a measure opening misses the floor by 0.024 units. **That is a hair,
and it is not a designed margin.** If any of the three widths involved changes,
in Finale Maestro or in a face someone swaps in, the flat crosses into the
middle case and gives back part of its gap. That is the rule working, not
breaking, but it is worth knowing the flat sits on the edge of it.

## 7. NOT ESTABLISHED

- **That 0.2 stave-spaces is right.** It is Dann's number, ruled 2026-09-02, and
  it carries no rule number. The Gould extraction is not on this machine, so I
  cannot say whether the book gives a figure for the air inside a bracketed
  accidental, and I did not look for one.
- **That the gap reads at the page's own size.** At 12x it is unmistakable. At
  the stave's own 5.5 px `lineGap` the gap is 1.1 px, and **whether that reads as
  air or as an artifact of the raster is Dann's eye, not a measurement.** The 3x
  panel in the screenshot is the closest thing here to the page's own size.
- **That half on each side is the right way to close it.** JUDGEMENT, given in
  §1. Closing the left gap first would keep the right side at full width for
  longer, and nobody has ruled between the two.
- **What happens at a face whose parenthesis boxes carry their own side
  bearings.** All three fonts the project ships put `bBoxSW[0]` at or within
  0.003 spaces of zero, so the parenthesis ink starts at its origin and the gap
  the renderer adds is the whole gap. A face that padded its boxes would show
  more air than 0.2 spaces, and this change would not know.
- **The measure-opening courtesy sharp**, §4's middle case, is arithmetic here
  and has not been read on a page. It is pinned by a test against a synthetic
  font, not by a walk.
- **Whether the flat's 0.024 units of headroom matters.** §4 records it as a
  hair. Nothing depends on it today.
- **That the gap should not also reach the required accidental.** It does not,
  and nothing asked it to. A required accidental has no parentheses, so there is
  nothing to separate; this is noted only because the two now compute their `gx`
  from arithmetic that is no longer identical.
